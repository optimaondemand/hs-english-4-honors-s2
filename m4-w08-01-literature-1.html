Week 8
The Gifts and the River
The leaving of Lórien, the gifts, and the divided counsel on the Great River.
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Hospitality ends in gifts, and the gifts leave the choice open.
Lothlórien sends the Company away with objects fitted to each member, and the river carries them toward a decision no one wants to make. The chapters show a Company that is still together and already divided in counsel.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can explain how the gifts of Lothlórien fit the people who receive them.
2
I can describe the divided counsel about the road and who holds each view.
3
I can trace how the river journey raises the pressure toward a choice.
📋 READING
▶
Read Book II, Chapter 8, Farewell to Lórien, and Chapter 9, The Great River, in your copy.
📚 PART 1 · THE LAST COUNSEL

On the last night Celeborn tells the Company that those who wish to turn back may stay for a while, and Galadriel observes that all have resolved to go forward. Boromir states his own course: it lies onward, toward Minas Tirith. Celeborn asks whether the whole Company is bound for Minas Tirith, and Aragorn answers that the course is undecided.

Celeborn then puts the choice in terms of the river. The way to Minas Tirith lies on the western shore, and the straight road of the quest lies on the eastern. He offers boats and names who can handle them, and the offer postpones the decision for some days. Aragorn is relieved.

Later the Company debates in its pavilion and reaches no conclusion. Most want to go first to Minas Tirith. Aragorn is divided between his own plan to go with Boromir to Gondor and the burden left to him by Gandalf, and Frodo says nothing. Boromir argues that it is folly to enter Mordor without force, and then he stops himself in mid-thought:

It would be folly to throw lives away, I mean. Frodo notices that the corrected sentence does not match the thought that came first, and he wonders what Boromir meant to say he should not throw away.

⚡ FIRST READ
Which gift looks best matched to the person who receives it?
📚 PART 2 · THE GIFTS

In the morning the Elves bring practical gifts for the whole Company: thin cakes of waybread that strengthen a traveller for a day, cloaks that blend with leaf and stone, and strong light ropes. The cloaks are not magic. The leader of the Elves says they are garments, not armour, but they help their wearers keep out of unfriendly eyes. Three grey boats wait at the landing.

At the parting feast on the river Galadriel gives each member a personal gift. The gifts fit the person, and except for Legolas’s bow they are tokens, not weapons against the road.

Galadriel is exact about what the gifts cannot do. Of Sam’s box she says It will not keep you on your road, nor defend you against any peril, and she adds that it may reward him if he sees his home again. The gifts honour and sustain. They do not make the choice for the traveller.

📚 PART 3 · THE PARTING AND THE RIVER

The Company sails away down the Silverlode into the Great River, and Galadriel stands alone on the bank. Gimli weeps and says to Legolas that he has seen the last of what was fairest. He admits that the danger he feared was torment in the dark, and then adds a harder confession: I would not have come, had I known the danger of light and joy. Legolas counts him blessed, since he chose this loss freely and has not forsaken his companions. Gimli answers that memory is a poor comfort, only a mirror of the thing.

Chapter 9 describes the voyage as drift. The Company lets the current carry it, because the decision at Rauros is still some days off and no one hurries toward it. Aragorn insists on early starts and late stops, because he fears the Dark Lord has not been idle. The lands change from woods to the Brown Lands to the stony hills of the Emyn Muil, and the mood falls with them.

Strains show on the boats. Boromir mutters, bites his nails, and looks long at Frodo. Sam sees a log with eyes. Aragorn confirms that it is Gollum, who has followed since Moria, and calls him our little footpad. At Sarn Gebir the Company, drifting at night, narrowly misses the rapids. Orcs shoot from the eastern bank, and Frodo’s mail turns an arrow. A winged shape comes out of the south and Legolas brings it down with the bow of Lórien. Frodo says only that It was something colder.

📚 PART 4 · THE ROAD DIVIDES

The next day Boromir argues that the Company should abandon the boats at the Emyn Muil and go west toward his own country. Aragorn will not give up the river, which cannot be missed, and he wishes to stand on the high seat of Amon Hen before he chooses. Boromir yields when it is clear that Frodo will follow Aragorn, and says he will go with them as far as the tall isle and then turn home. His reason is a point of honour: It is not the way of the Men of Minas Tirith to desert their friends at need.

The Company carries the boats around the rapids along an old portage-way and passes between the great stone kings of the Argonath. Frodo sees Aragorn change: Strider, and yet not Strider. The ranger speaks as an heir returning to his own land, and then falls into doubt: But whither now shall I go?

The chapter ends on the lake of Nen Hithoel at the foot of Tol Brandir. The narrator states the situation plainly: They could go no further without choice between the east-way and the west.

⚡ TRY IT · WHAT DOES THE EVIDENCE SHOW?
Choose the reading the text best supports.
1. Galadriel tells Sam that his box will not keep him on his road or defend him against any peril. What does this say about the Elves’ gifts?
2. Boromir corrects himself from “throw away” to “throw lives away”. What does Frodo notice?
3. Gimli says he would not have come had he known the danger of light and joy. Which reading is best supported?
🔑 KEY TERMS
counsel: advice, or a joint decision about what to do.
portage: the carrying of boats and goods overland between waters.
token: a small object that stands for a person, a bond, or a hope.
