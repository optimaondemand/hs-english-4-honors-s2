📋 READING
▶
Read Chapter 4 of Book II, A Journey in the Dark, in your copy.
📚 PART 1 · THE ONLY ROAD

On the evening after the failed pass, Gandalf lays the choice out: We have no choice but to go on, or to return. To return would be to admit defeat, he says, and Rivendell would later be besieged and destroyed. The only way left is the road under the mountain. Boromir objects to the name Moria and proposes the Gap of Rohan, and Gandalf replies that the Ring must not come near Isengard. He ends with a principle: one must tread the path that need chooses.

Aragorn gives his consent, and he warns Gandalf to beware. Boromir refuses unless the whole Company votes against him. Frodo asks that no vote be taken until morning. The wolves settle the matter, since the Company is attacked on the hill that night and Gandalf drives them off with fire. Necessity again decides what the debate could not.

⚡ FIRST READ
Gandalf says the only road is under the mountain. Why does he call it a road chosen by need?
📚 PART 2 · THE DOOR AND THE COST

The west door of Moria stands beyond a lake that the Gate-stream has been dammed to fill. Gandalf tries many spells on the shut doors and fails. When he sees that the inscription on the arch was a password written plainly, the doors open: the Elvish word for friend was the answer, and the line that seemed to say speak, friend had meant say friend. The riddle was too simple for a learned reader.

Something in the water seizes Frodo by the ankle. Sam cuts at it, the Company runs for the stair, and the doors are slammed shut behind them by the creature’s arms. One loss follows from the other. Bill, the pony Sam had cared for since Bree, had to be sent away before the door, because he could not enter the Mines. Gandalf told Sam he must choose between the pony and his master. Afterward Sam says: I had to choose, Mr. Frodo. I had to come with you. The lesson of the scene is that a choice made for a purpose can still cost something the chooser loves.

📚 PART 3 · THE DARK AND THE FOLLOWER

Gandalf leads, with Gimli beside him, and the others follow. Aragorn tells them not to be afraid: he will lead us out again, at whatever cost to himself. The line looks forward, and it also sets a price on the leader.

Frodo, whose senses are sharper since the wound, hears the faint fall of soft bare feet behind the Company. It continues when the Company halts and stops only after a pause. Later, on watch, he glimpses two pale points of light. The chapter does not name the follower. A reader who remembers Book I will suspect Gollum, and Aragorn confirms it much later in the volume, saying that the creature padded after them all through Moria.

The follower connects to an earlier decision. In Book I, Gandalf told Frodo that Bilbo’s pity had stayed his hand when he could have killed Gollum, and that the pity of Bilbo might rule the fate of many. Moria shows what that pity has left alive in the world: a creature whose footsteps haunt the Company. Pity spared him, and now he follows.

The chapter ends at the tomb of Balin, lord of Moria. The tomb is a closed door of another kind: the dwarf colony that tried to retake Moria is dead, and Gimli covers his face.

⚡ TRY IT
Choose the reading the chapter best supports.
1. What does the author gain by leaving the follower unnamed in this chapter?
2. Why does Gandalf call returning to Rivendell a defeat?
🔑 KEY TERMS
necessity: something that must be done because no other course remains.
dread: a deep fear of something that is expected but not yet seen.
