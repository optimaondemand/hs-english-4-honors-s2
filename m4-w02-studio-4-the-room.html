The Room
The ask, the questions an investor will put, and the final deck.
🎯 TODAY’S FOCUS
The deck is built and the three-minute run is recorded. Session 4 prepares for the room itself: making a clear ask, anticipating the questions an investor will put, and answering them briefly and honestly. The Final Pitch is its own graded Studio item with its own page. About 30 minutes a week goes to this work.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can state an ask in terms of what is wanted and what the investor receives.
2
I can predict the four kinds of question an investor puts and prepare an answer to each.
3
I can answer in three parts: the answer, the evidence, and the limit.
💭 WARM-UP
An investor asks a question the speaker cannot fully answer. What does the strongest speaker do?
📖 PART 1 · THE ASK

The sixth slide says what the speaker wants and what the investor receives in return. Both halves are specific. “Support for my idea” is vague. “A four-week pilot with two classrooms, in return for the results and the right to expand” can be weighed.

An ask is a claim like any other, and it follows the same rule: only what can be defended. The amount, the time and the promised result all have to match the evidence on the slides before it.

📖 PART 2 · FOUR KINDS OF QUESTION

Investors vary, but their questions fall into four groups. Preparing one answer for each group covers most of the room.

KindThe question in plain wordsA sample
MarketWho needs this, and who pays?“Why would a school buy this instead of using what it has?”
EvidenceHow is it known to work?“How do you know students keep using it after the first week?”
RiskWhat goes wrong?“What happens if the AI gives a student a wrong answer?”
FitWhy this speaker and why now?“What changed that makes this possible this year?”
📖 PART 3 · ANSWER, EVIDENCE, LIMIT

A strong answer runs three short steps: the answer in one sentence, the evidence behind it, and the limit of that evidence. Naming the limit is what makes the answer believable.

Sample question: “How do you know students keep using it after the first week?”
Weak: “They love it. Everyone I showed it to wanted more.”
Strong: “Most do. Four of the five testers opened it again the next week. Five is a small test, so the next step is a month-long trial with thirty students.”
Answer, then evidence, then limit, in about twenty seconds.
📖 PART 4 · FINAL DECK POLISH
☐
Six slides in order: Hook, Problem, App, Why It Works, Story, Ask.
☐
One idea on each slide, with few words and a consistent design theme.
☐
Every number on a slide has a source that can be named aloud.
☐
The Ask slide states what is wanted and what the investor receives.
☐
Speaker notes are current and match what is said in the recording.
☐
The file is saved in the English IV Senior Studios folder under the final file name.
🤖 WORKING WITH CLAUDE
Claude can play a skeptical investor. Paste the deck text or script and ask for one tough question per slide, with no suggested answers. Claude can ask questions an actual investor would not, and it can miss the one a particular investor would. Treat its questions as practice only. The speaker writes each answer, checks every fact in it, and delivers it aloud.

🔑 KEY TERMS
ask: what the speaker wants and what the investor receives.
skeptic’s question: a question meant to find the weakest claim in the pitch.
limit: the honest edge of the evidence: what it does not show.
