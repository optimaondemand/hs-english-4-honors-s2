Week 4
Strider
A stranger judged by his looks, and the long watch behind them.
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Strider is distrusted by the Bree-folk, by Butterbur, and at first by Frodo and Sam.
Gandalf’s letter, a broken sword, and a short verse show what the hobbits could not see. The chapters then show the watch Strider has kept, and goes on keeping, without thanks.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can explain how Strider’s appearance and manner shape the hobbits’ first judgment of him.
2
I can use the verse in Gandalf’s letter to explain the gap between how Strider looks and what he is.
3
I can trace what Strider does between the inn and the Ford and say what it shows about his duty.
📋 READING
▶
Read Book I, Chapter 10, Strider, Chapter 11, A Knife in the Dark, and Chapter 12, Flight to the Ford, in your copy.
📚 PART 1 · A STRANGER IN THE CORNER

The first description of Strider is a list of marks that make him look suspect: a weather-beaten man in the shadows, boots caked with mud, and a hood drawn over his face despite the heat of the room. Butterbur says only that he is one of the wandering folk called Rangers, that he seldom talks, and that nobody knows his right name. The narrator adds that the Bree-folk never made friends of the Rangers.

In the parlour Strider names a price for his help: to be taken along until he chooses to leave. He also says aloud what the hobbits are thinking: Well, I have rather a rascally look, have I not?

Sam objects that a man out of the Wild is no one to trust. Butterbur, arriving to confess that he forgot a letter, says he would not take up with a Ranger himself. Frodo reasons more carefully. He does not yet trust Strider, but he notices that Strider’s voice has changed from the Bree-folk’s way of speaking, and that the fear Strider causes is not the kind an enemy’s servant would cause.

⚡ FIRST READ
Why do the hobbits hold back from trusting Strider?
📚 PART 2 · THE LETTER, THE SWORD, AND THE VERSE

Gandalf’s letter, written at the inn in midsummer and never sent, tells Frodo to leave Bag End soon, to trust the landlord, and to expect a friend on the Road: a Man, lean, dark and tall, called Strider. A postscript warns Frodo to make sure it is the real Strider, since many strange men are on the roads, and gives his true name, Aragorn. Below it Gandalf writes a short verse.

All that is gold does not glitter,
Not all those who wander are lost;

The first two lines set appearance against worth. The last two turn to a broken sword and a crownless king: Renewed shall be blade that was broken, The crownless again shall be king. Later in the scene, after Sam challenges him, Strider gives his name as Aragorn son of Arathorn and draws a sword broken a foot below the hilt.

The proof the hobbits accept, however, is conduct more than verse. Sam asks how they can know Strider is the real one, and Strider answers that if he had killed the real Strider he could kill Sam, and that if he were after the Ring he could take it at once. An enemy with that power would not stop to argue. Frodo reasons that a spy would seem fairer and feel fouler than Strider does, and Strider restates the point as a reversal: I look foul and feel fair.

📚 PART 3 · A WATCH KEPT UNSEEN

Strider explains his reserve in practical and slightly sad terms. He needed to study the hobbits first, because the Enemy has set traps for him before, and he adds: A hunted man sometimes wearies of distrust and longs for friendship.

The chapters that follow show what the watch consists of. He has guarded the borders of the Shire for years while Gandalf was busy elsewhere. He leads the hobbits off the Road by a winding route through the Chetwood and the Midgewater Marshes toward Weathertop, and on the hill he admits afterward that three of them standing in view was a mistake. When the Riders come by night he drives them off with firebrands. When Frodo is wounded he searches in the dark for a healing plant called athelas and treats the wound, though he says its power may be small. He then takes the party the long way to the Ford, with Frodo on the pony, until Glorfindel arrives from Rivendell and takes Frodo on his own horse.

Strider does not sleep through these nights. At Bree, as the party leaves, Bill Ferny jeers at him with a nickname while most of the crowd draws back from him. The hobbits’ first judgment by looks is not corrected by anyone in the village. The correction comes from what Strider does.

⚡ TRY IT
Choose the reading the text best supports.
1. How does the verse beginning “All that is gold does not glitter” apply to Strider at Bree?
2. Strider tells Sam that if he were after the Ring he could take it now. What does the answer show?
🔑 KEY TERMS
antithesis: a contrast set up in balanced wording, as in “gold” against “glitter”.
token: a sign or pledge that shows something to be true.
