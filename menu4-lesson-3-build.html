Who came, and by what road
The Council of Elrond, person by person
●
Elrond says the people at the Council were not summoned by him, and that their meeting is no accident. He also says the burden is too heavy for anyone to lay on another. The table lists what the chapter shows for each person.
WhoWhat brings them to the roomWhat the chapter shows them doing
FrodoHe carried the Ring out of the Shire, and Elrond presents him to the Council.He speaks only after a long silence, and he offers to take the Ring though he does not know the way.
SamHe followed Frodo to Rivendell and sat in a corner uninvited.He cries out against sending Frodo alone, and Elrond answers that he will go too.
AragornHe is a Ranger and a descendant of Isildur, and he is long used to guarding people who do not thank him.He shows the broken sword of his line and says he will come to Minas Tirith.
BoromirHe took the errand on himself after a dream that came to his brother, and once to him, to ask the meaning of its riddle.He argues that the Ring could be Gondor’s weapon, and he bows his head when Elrond refuses.
Glóin (with his son Gimli)Glóin was sent with word that Sauron’s messengers were asking the Dwarves about a hobbit and his ring.He tells of Sauron’s offer and suggests joining the strengths of the free peoples.
LegolasHe was sent by his father with the news that Gollum had escaped.He gives the report and takes his place at the Council.
GandalfHe has gathered the Ring’s history and the news of the Shire, and Saruman held him captive.He tells the long tale and argues that the Ring must be destroyed.
●
Read down the middle column. Some reasons are personal, some are loyalties to a people, and some answer something higher. A strong answer asks which of these reasons would be enough by itself.
QUICK CHECK · NOT GRADED

Quick Write: Answering the Call
Week 5 · 20 points · turned in as this week’s PDF
●
This is the whole Quick Write. Write it in the box at the end of this section. The teacher scores this box.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Write 300 to 400 words explaining how the people at the Council of Elrond come to the burden of the Ring by different roads, and why the call is neither a free choice nor an inheritance.

●
Focus question. What makes a call a call, when no one picks it like a prize and no one can hand it down like a title?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Choose three people at the Council (Frodo, Sam, Aragorn, Boromir, Glóin or Gimli, Legolas, Gandalf). In a phrase each, name what brought that person to the room.
2
Paraphrase what Elrond says about the gathering and about who can take up the burden.
3
Explain why the call cannot be reduced to a free choice, and why it cannot be reduced to an inheritance.
4
State what answering the call asks beyond a private wish or loyalty to one’s own people.
5
Use one short sentence of fewer than ten words for the core claim, and one longer sentence whose subject and verb stay easy to find. Use two of this week’s Word Study words.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
Three people are named, each with what brought them to the Council.
☐
Elrond’s words are paraphrased accurately.
☐
The piece explains why neither free choice nor inheritance accounts for the call.
☐
The piece states what the call asks beyond a private wish or loyalty to one’s own people.
☐
One short core sentence, one longer sentence with a clear core, and two Word Study words are used; the writing is 300 to 400 words.
HOW IT’S SCORED
Criteria Full credit Partial Pts
The call explained
8 pts		 The piece shows why the call is neither a free choice nor an inheritance and what it asks beyond private wish or loyalty to a people. The piece describes the three people but does not explain the call. 8
Use of the Council chapter
8 pts		 Three people with their reasons, and Elrond’s words, are accurate and used as evidence. Reasons are general, one person is missing, or Elrond’s words are misstated. 8
Craft targets
4 pts		 One short core sentence, one longer sentence with a clear core, and two Word Study words used accurately; 300 to 400 words. One target is missed, or the length is off. 4
Total: 20 pts

The test
Three questions that separate the cases
●
Self-sacrifice and self-destruction can end the same way. The difference lies in who chooses, who is served, and what the act is for.
The testGandalf at the Bridge (Book II, Chapter 5)
Is it chosen?He tells the Company to flee and holds the narrow span himself.
Who is served?The Company reaches the open air while the Balrog is held at the bridge.
What does he keep, and what is lost?He breaks the bridge, and the Balrog’s whip drags him into the abyss; his last words order the others to run.
Your case: Gregor or BoromirAsk the same three questions of Gregor’s end, or of Boromir’s argument at the Council.
●
A test only works if it can sort a hard case. Try it on Gregor or on Boromir before deciding that it holds.
QUICK CHECK · NOT GRADED
Which detail from the Bridge most supports calling Gandalf’s stand self-sacrifice instead of self-destruction?

Quick Write: Sacrifice or Self-Destruction?
Week 6 · 20 points · turned in as this week’s PDF
●
This is the whole Quick Write. Write it in the box at the end of this section. The teacher scores this box.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Write 300 to 400 words that separate self-sacrifice from self-destruction, using Gandalf’s stand at the Bridge of Khazad-dûm and one contrast: Gregor’s end in The Metamorphosis, or Boromir’s argument at the Council that the Ring could serve as a weapon.

●
Focus question. What separates giving oneself for a purpose from losing oneself?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
State a test in the first sentence: the one condition that separates self-sacrifice from self-destruction.
2
Apply the test to Gandalf. Describe what he does at the Bridge, what the Company can do because of it, and whether he chooses it.
3
Apply the test to your contrast, Gregor’s end or Boromir’s argument, and say which side of the line it falls on.
4
State the best reason someone could place one of these cases on the opposite side of the line, and respond.
5
Use at least one clause of cause and one clause of concession (because, since, although, even though), and two of this week’s Word Study words.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
The first sentence states one test that separates the two.
☐
The test is applied to Gandalf with specific events from the Bridge.
☐
The test is applied to Gregor or Boromir, and the case is placed on one side of the line.
☐
The best opposing reading is stated and answered.
☐
At least one clause of cause, one clause of concession and two Word Study words are used accurately; the writing is 300 to 400 words.
HOW IT’S SCORED
Criteria Full credit Partial Pts
The test and its application
8 pts		 A clear test is applied to Gandalf and to the contrast, and each case is placed with reasons. The test is vague, or it is applied to only one case. 8
Evidence and the objection
8 pts		 Specific events from the Bridge and from the contrast are explained, and the best opposing reading is answered. Events are retold, or the opposing reading is missing or thin. 8
Craft targets
4 pts		 At least one clause of cause, one clause of concession and two Word Study words used accurately; 300 to 400 words. One target is missed, or the length is off. 4
Total: 20 pts
Sacrifice or Self-Destruction?: argument (300 to 400 words)
One argument of 300 to 400 words. Check it against the success criteria above before downloading.

Build the core
The first piece of the project, matched to the format
●
The core states the claim and shows the first piece of evidence at work. The rest of the project grows from it.
ANALYTICAL OR SYNTHESIS ESSAY
Write the thesis paragraph: the claim, the two or three texts that carry it, and the strongest counterargument in one line. Then list four parts, each with the evidence it will use.
DEBATE
Write the opening speech for the side that seems weaker, at full strength. The second side and the judgment come in the draft.
LETTERS OR DIARY
Choose the character and the person written to. Write the first letter or diary page in full, and list the moments the later pieces will use.
SHORT FILM OR SCREENPLAY
Write the logline and the opening scene (about one page, or one minute). List the three images or sounds that will carry the interpretation.
PODCAST OR ORATION
Write the opening minute word for word, with the claim in it. Mark the passage that will be read aloud.

Three cases
What the offer touched, and what it found
●
Galadriel names her own temptation. Boromir hints at his. Gollum made up a defense for his. The table sets the three side by side, and the last row is for the reader’s own judgment.
GaladrielBoromirGollum
What touches the personFrodo offers her the Ring, and she admits she has long imagined having it.He says the Lady seemed to tempt him and will not say what she offered. At the Council he urged the Ring as a weapon.Sméagol’s friend Déagol finds the ring on Sméagol’s birthday.
What the person doesShe describes the Queen she would become, then refuses, and shrinks to a simple figure in white.He refuses to listen to the offer, by his own account, and warns the others to take care.He strangles Déagol and later invents a defense that the ring was his birthday gift.
Reveals or creates? (your reading)What was in her before the offer?What was in him before the offer?What was in him before the offer?
●
Connotation matters in this argument. A word such as firm or stubborn names the same act and passes a different judgment.

Quick Write: The Mirror Test
Week 7 · 20 points · turned in as this week’s PDF
●
This is the whole Quick Write. Write it in the box at the end of this section. The teacher scores this box.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Write 300 to 400 words that answer this question: does temptation reveal a corruption already present, or create it? Use Galadriel at the Mirror and either Boromir or Gollum.

●
Focus question. What was in a person before the Ring was offered, and what did the offer change?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
State a claim in the first sentence: temptation reveals, creates, or does both.
2
Use Galadriel. Explain what she admits wanting when Frodo offers the Ring, and what she does.
3
Use Boromir (his remarks about the Lady and his argument at the Council) or Gollum (Sméagol and Déagol). Say what the temptation shows about him.
4
Answer the other side: state the best case for the opposite claim, and respond.
5
Choose words for their connotation. Use at least two precise word choices (for example, firm or stubborn), and explain one of them in a clause. Use two of this week’s Word Study words.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
The first sentence makes a claim about whether temptation reveals or creates.
☐
Galadriel’s admission and refusal are described accurately.
☐
Boromir or Gollum is used as a second case, with a specific moment from the reading.
☐
The best opposing case is stated and answered.
☐
At least two precise word choices, one explained, and two Word Study words are used; the writing is 300 to 400 words.
HOW IT’S SCORED
Criteria Full credit Partial Pts
Claim and reasoning
8 pts		 A clear claim about revealing or creating, supported by what each person already was before the offer. The claim is vague, or the reasoning describes the scenes without testing the claim. 8
Use of the chapters and the other side
8 pts		 Galadriel and a second case are described accurately, and the best opposing case is answered. One case is missing or misstated, or the opposing case is not answered. 8
Craft targets
4 pts		 At least two precise word choices, one explained, and two Word Study words used accurately; 300 to 400 words. One target is missed, or the length is off. 4
Total: 20 pts
