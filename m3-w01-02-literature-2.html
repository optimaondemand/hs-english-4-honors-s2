📚 PART 1 · THE ARRANGEMENT

Gregor is a traveling salesman who carries cloth samples. He stays in the post to pay off a debt that his parents owe the owner of the firm. By his own estimate five or six more years will clear it. Until then he is bound to work he dislikes, with early trains, irregular meals, and no lasting friendships. In five years with the firm he has not been ill once.

The arrangement is not presented as a grievance to be fixed. Gregor treats it as what a son owes. His private plan to give notice waits on the debt, so the job governs both his days and his imagined future.

⚡ FIRST READ
Why does Gregor stay in a job he dislikes?
📚 PART 2 · THE FIRM AT THE DOOR

Gregor oversleeps and misses the five o’clock train. A little after seven the chief clerk himself is at the Samsa flat. About two hours after one missed train, the firm has sent its most senior clerk to the home of a salesman with five years of service, and Gregor reads the visit as what it is: an inspection.

The clerk speaks through the locked door. He says the firm had first suspected something to do with money recently entrusted to Gregor, that he half defended him, and that his recent sales have been unsatisfactory. He says all of this in front of the parents. He also remarks that people in business are expected to work through minor illness for the firm’s sake. No one at the door asks about Gregor’s health in a way that matters to him.

Gregor answers with a long, anxious speech from the floor: he is dizzy, he will take the eight o’clock train, the clerk should speak well of him to the boss. The speech shows what the arrangement has trained him to do. He defends his record before he has looked at his own condition.

🔑 KEY TERMS
function: the use a person or thing serves; a worker is treated as a function when valued only for what he does.
obligation: a duty a person feels bound to carry out, whether or not he chose it.
impersonal: treating individuals as cases or units rather than as persons.
