Your craft standard
What excellent looks like in the chosen format
ANALYTICAL OR SYNTHESIS ESSAY
A thesis stated up front. Logical structure. Quotations woven in: frame, quote, explain. A real counterargument, answered. Clean academic prose. About 900 to 1,300 words.
DEBATE
Both sides argued at full strength and fairly. Each side grounded in the texts. A reasoned judgment that earns its conclusion. Written, or recorded as a spoken piece.
LETTERS OR DIARY
A consistent, convincing voice for each writer. Every letter or entry tied to a real moment in a text. A clear arc that reveals an interpretation, closed by a short analytical afterword of about 150 to 250 words.
SHORT FILM OR SCREENPLAY
Deliberate choices (framing, pacing, dialogue, sound) that carry the interpretation. A coherent scene or short film, or a screenplay of four to six pages. Production quality that fits student work.
PODCAST OR ORATION
A clear spoken argument. Strong delivery (pace, emphasis, clarity). Awareness of the audience. At least one quoted passage read aloud to real effect. About 4 to 7 minutes.

Plato’s cave and the Shire
Test the picture before using it
●
Plato’s prisoners take shadows for the whole world. The Shire is not a prison, so the picture has to be tested, not assumed. Four questions show where it fits and where it does not.
PLATO, THE CAVE
Behold! human beings living in an underground den, which has a mouth open towards the light and reaching all along the den; here they have been from their childhood, and have their legs and necks chained so that they cannot move...
Plato, Republic VII (Jowett translation; public domain)
In Plato’s caveAsk of the Shire
The prisoners see only shadows on a wall.What does the Shire know about the world beyond its borders, and who brings that news?
The chains hold, and the prisoners do not know they are chained.What keeps hobbits from looking outward: force, or habit and contentment?
A freed prisoner is pained by the light and slow to see.How does Frodo take the news Gandalf brings in the chapter on the Shadow of the Past?
A prisoner who returns is laughed at, and the others would put to death anyone who tried to lead them up.How does the Shire regard Bilbo and his adventures, and what would hobbits make of Gandalf’s account?
THE ONE WHO RETURNS
...and if anyone tried to loose another and lead him up to the light, let them only catch the offender, and they would put him to death.
Plato, Republic VII (Jowett translation; public domain)
●
Plato’s point concerns habits of mind, not walls. A claim that the Shire is a cave has to say what its comfort keeps hobbits from seeing.
QUICK CHECK · NOT GRADED
A student writes: “The Shire is a cave because hobbits are comfortable.” Which revision is the most arguable and the most faithful to Plato?

Quick Write: Comfort or Cave?
Week 1 · 20 points · turned in as this week’s PDF
●
This is the whole Quick Write. Write it in the box at the end of this section. The teacher scores this box.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Write one argument of 300 to 400 words that answers this question: is the comfort of the Shire a kind of cave?

●
Focus question. What does comfort cost a community that never looks past its borders?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
State a claim in the first sentence: the Shire is, or is not, a cave in Plato’s sense.
2
Use Plato. Name one feature of the cave (the shadows, the chains, the painful turn toward the light, the mockery of the one who returns) and apply it to the Shire, or test it against the Shire.
3
Use the reading. Choose one moment from the Prologue or the first two chapters and explain what it shows about how the Shire knows, or does not know, the wider world.
4
Answer the other side. State the strongest reason the Shire’s comfort is harmless or good, then respond.
5
Use at least one appositive and two of this week’s Word Study words, each used accurately.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
The first sentence makes a clear claim about the Shire and the cave.
☐
One feature of the cave is described correctly and applied to the Shire.
☐
One moment from the reading is explained, not retold.
☐
The strongest opposing reason is stated fairly and answered.
☐
At least one appositive and two Word Study words are used accurately, and the writing is 300 to 400 words.
HOW IT’S SCORED
Criteria Full credit Partial Pts
Claim and use of Plato
8 pts		 A clear claim; the cave is described correctly and applied to the Shire with reasoning. The claim is vague, or the cave is named without being applied. 8
Evidence and the other side
8 pts		 One moment from the reading is explained, and the strongest opposing reason is answered. The evidence is retold rather than explained, or the opposing view is missing or thin. 8
Craft targets
4 pts		 At least one appositive and two Word Study words used accurately; 300 to 400 words. One target is missed, or the length is off. 4
Total: 20 pts

Two kinds of care
A comparison to test
●
The friends and the Samsa household both act out of care. The difference shows in three places.
The hobbits’ conspiracy (Book I, Chapter 5)The Samsa household (Part II)
Who knows the truth?Merry, Pippin and Sam know about the Ring and the danger, and they say so once the plan is unmasked.Grete brings food to learn what Gregor will eat, but he cannot tell her anything, and the household speaks of him as a problem.
Who sets the limits?Frodo decides the route, through the Old Forest. The friends supply ponies, stores and company.The family decides the room, the door and who may see him.
What stays in the person’s hands?The decision about the road and the pace.Very little. Gregor hides under the sofa to spare Grete the sight of him, and nobody asks what he would choose.
●
Care can protect a person and still take the decision away. The strongest comparison names the one condition that separates the two.

Quick Write: Care That Frees
Week 2 · 20 points · turned in as this week’s PDF
●
This is the whole Quick Write. Write it in the box at the end of this section. The teacher scores this box.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Write one comparison of 250 to 350 words: the care Merry, Pippin and Sam show Frodo in The Fellowship of the Ring against the care the Samsa family gives Gregor in The Metamorphosis.

●
Focus question. What separates care that frees a person from care that closes the door?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
State in the first sentence what makes care free a person and what makes it confine.
2
Give one moment from Book I, Chapter 5 where the hobbits’ care leaves a decision with Frodo, and say what it leaves him.
3
Give one moment from Part II of The Metamorphosis where the household’s care narrows Gregor’s life, and say what it takes from him.
4
Name the one condition that makes the difference (for example, who knows the truth, or who sets the limits).
5
Use at least two participial phrases and two of this week’s Word Study words, each used accurately.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
The first sentence defines care that frees and care that confines.
☐
A specific moment from each book is explained, not retold.
☐
One condition is named as the difference, and the comparison supports it.
☐
At least two participial phrases and two Word Study words are used accurately, and the writing is 250 to 350 words.
HOW IT’S SCORED
Criteria Full credit Partial Pts
Comparison of the two kinds of care
8 pts		 Care that frees and care that confines are defined, and one condition is named as the difference. The two kinds of care are described but not defined, or no condition is named. 8
Evidence from both books
8 pts		 A specific moment from each book is explained and tied to the comparison. One moment is missing, or the moments are retold without explanation. 8
Craft targets
4 pts		 At least two participial phrases and two Word Study words used accurately; 250 to 350 words. One target is missed, or the length is off. 4
Total: 20 pts
Care That Frees: comparison (250 to 350 words)
One comparison of 250 to 350 words. Check it against the success criteria above before downloading.
