📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
A refuge admits strangers only on terms, and the Company arrives carrying a loss.
Lothlórien welcomes the Company and guards itself in the same hour. The chapter shows how hospitality and caution can sit together, and the next chapter shows the Company grieving in the place that took it in.

Read Book II, Chapter 6, Lothlórien, in your copy. Then read Chapter 7, The Mirror of Galadriel, as far as the Company’s first night in the pavilion.
🏛 SETTING
Lothlórien lies between two rivers, the Silverlode and the Great River. Its Elves are the Galadhrim, the Tree-people, and they keep watch from wooden platforms in the mallorn trees.
Celeborn and Galadriel rule it. The Company arrives in winter with eight members: four hobbits, two Men, an Elf, and a Dwarf. Gandalf was lost in Moria.
No Dwarf had entered the wood in a long age, and the Elves of the wood had little dealing with other peoples.
📚 PART 1 · THE EDGE OF THE WOOD

Chapter 6 opens at the Dimrill Dale. Aragorn grieves for Gandalf, then turns the Company toward the road. Gimli stops at the Mirrormere to look at Durin’s Stone, and Aragorn tends the hurts of Frodo and Sam in a dell. Under Frodo’s jacket he finds a mail-coat of mithril, which explains why Frodo survived the blow in Moria.

At nightfall the Company reaches the eaves of the Golden Wood. Boromir holds back. He repeats what Gondor says of the place: few who go in come out, and none unharmed. Aragorn corrects the word:

Say not unscathed, but if you say unchanged, then maybe you will speak the truth, and he adds that Gondor’s lore must be failing if its people now speak evil of the wood.

He closes the argument with a statement about the place and about the visitor: fair and perilous; but only evil need fear it, or those who bring some evil with them. The sentence makes the wood a test of what a traveller carries in, and the next chapter applies it to named characters.

⚡ FIRST READ
Which statement best describes how the Elves treat the Company on the first night?
📚 PART 2 · HOSPITALITY WITH CONDITIONS

The Elves have known of the Company for some time. They speak from the branches, and Legolas answers them. Frodo and Sam climb a rope ladder to a platform where Haldir waits. He speaks to them in the Common Language and says that they do not look evil. He accepts them because Elrond asked and because they come with an Elf of his northern kindred. He then asks how many there are and learns of the Dwarf, whom the law of the wood does not admit. He yields only on terms: Aragorn and Legolas must answer for Gimli, and Gimli must go blindfold.

The next morning Haldir guides the Company to the river Celebrant and sets up ropes as a bridge, then names the blindfold again at the border of the Naith. Gimli refuses and lays a hand on his axe. Haldir answers that he does not doubt Gimli and still cannot set the law aside. Aragorn ends the quarrel by having every member of the Company blindfolded, Legolas included.

📚 PART 3 · WHY THE BLINDFOLD

Haldir gives a reason that reaches beyond the dispute with Gimli. He says that in nothing is the power of the Dark Lord more clearly shown than in the estrangement that divides all those who still oppose him. The Elves keep the law because trust has grown rare, and they cannot risk their land on a stranger’s word.

He describes the cost to his own people with a contrast between two instruments: our hands are more often upon the bowstring than upon the harp. The bow and the harp stand for a life spent on defence against a life spent on music. The Elves are generous within a narrow ring of safety, and they know what the narrowness costs them.

The scene gives a second view of hospitality that the module has already met. In Bree the inn welcomed hobbits and eyed Strider with suspicion. In the Samsa household guests were welcomed while the son was kept out of sight. Lothlórien offers shelter and watchfulness together and names its reasons, and the Elves then act on a message that lifts the condition.

📚 PART 4 · GRIEF IN THE CITY OF THE TREES

In Caras Galadhon the Company is brought before Celeborn and Galadriel. Celeborn counts eight where the messages promised nine. Galadriel says that Gandalf never crossed the borders, and Aragorn tells her that he fell into shadow in Moria. The Elves cry out. Aragorn tells the full tale at Celeborn’s request, and Legolas names the Balrog.

Celeborn answers in sorrow and in blame. He says that, had he known the Dwarves had stirred the evil again, he would have forbidden the Company to pass the northern borders, and he suggests that Gandalf fell from wisdom into folly. Galadriel rebukes the suggestion: Needless were none of the deeds of Gandalf in life. She adds that the Company cannot report his full purpose, and that the followers are blameless. She then welcomes Gimli by naming the Dwarves’ old places in his own tongue. Celeborn withdraws his harsh words with the plain statement I spoke in the trouble of my heart.

Galadriel closes the audience with a warning and a condition: the quest is on the edge of a knife, and Yet hope remains while all the Company is true. In the days that follow the Elves sing laments for Gandalf, and Legolas will not translate them, for his grief is still too near for song. Frodo puts his own sorrow into a poem, but only snatches of it stay with him. It ends with Gandalf alone on the bridge. Frodo adds that he cannot face carrying the news to Bilbo.

🔑 KEY TERMS
estrangement: a division between people who ought to stand together.
kindred: a people or family joined by common descent.
lament: a song or poem of grief.
