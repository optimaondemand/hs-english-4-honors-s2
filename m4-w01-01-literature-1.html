📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
The book opens in a comfortable country, and the comfort is part of the story.
The Prologue presents hobbits as a people who like quiet, good food and gifts. Chapter 1 shows that world at its most pleasant, a birthday party, and then shows the party end in a departure and a ring passed to an heir.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can describe what the Prologue says hobbits value and what they have stopped noticing.
2
I can trace how the Ring moves from Bilbo to Frodo in Chapter 1.
3
I can explain why Tolkien begins with an ordinary person rather than a warrior or a king.

📜 HISTORICAL CONTEXT
J. R. R. Tolkien (1892–1973) was a scholar of Old English who taught at Oxford. He served as a soldier in the First World War, including at the Battle of the Somme in 1916, and two of his closest school friends were killed in that war. The Hobbit appeared in 1937. The Fellowship of the Ring came out in 1954 as the first of three volumes of The Lord of the Rings, which Tolkien had written as one long story.
📚 PART 1 · THE PEOPLE OF THE SHIRE

The Prologue reads like the notes of a patient historian. Hobbits are a small, quiet people who like well-farmed land, six meals a day, parties, and the giving of presents. They distrust machines more complicated than a water-mill, and they avoid the “Big Folk” by disappearing from the road. The Shire has almost no government. Families manage their own affairs, the old laws are kept freely because they are ancient and just, and the one real official, the mayor, mostly presides at banquets.

The narrator is fond of this people and also exact about them. Two details carry the weight. First, hobbits are tougher than their comfort suggests: the Prologue says that people who love good things can do without them when they must. Second, the long peace has made them forgetful:

They were, in fact, sheltered, but they had ceased to remember it. (Prologue)

The sentence turns the whole opening into a question. The Shire is safe, and the hobbits do not know who or what keeps it safe. The Mathom-house at Michel Delving shows the habit in small: it stores weapons and gifts that nobody uses and nobody will throw away.

⚡ FIRST READ
Which description best fits the Prologue’s attitude toward the hobbits?
📚 PART 2 · THE PARTY AND THE RING PASSED ON

Chapter 1 gathers the Shire into one field. Bilbo turns one hundred and eleven and Frodo turns thirty-three, the age at which a hobbit comes of age, on the same day. One hundred and forty-four guests eat through the afternoon, Gandalf supplies the fireworks, and the talk in the inn beforehand shows what the district thinks of Bilbo: rich, odd, and too well preserved to be natural.

The speech ends the party. Bilbo announces that he is leaving, puts on the Ring, and vanishes. Nearly every guest takes it for a bad joke. Frodo alone sits silent beside the empty chair, and the narrator says why: he realizes that he loves the old hobbit.

The private scene afterward is where the chapter changes tone. Bilbo means to leave the Ring to Frodo and cannot let go of it. When Gandalf presses him, his voice turns sharp, and he calls the ring by the name Gollum used:

It is mine, I tell you. My own. My Precious. Yes, my Precious. (Book I, Chapter 1)

Gandalf answers with anger of his own and then with an appeal to old trust. Bilbo yields, jokes that the party was meant to make the giving-away easier, and walks out into the night. Gandalf places the envelope on the mantelpiece, and later advises Frodo not to use the Ring at all and to keep it secret and safe. Frodo thinks it may prove useful.

⏱ TIMELINE · FROM THE FINDING TO THE PARTY (SHIRE-RECKONING)
S.R. 1341
Bilbo leaves Bag End with Gandalf and thirteen dwarves on a quest for dragon-gold.
Under the mountains
In the dark of the goblin tunnels he finds a ring, and wins a riddle-game with Gollum.
S.R. 1342
Bilbo returns to Bag End and keeps the ring secret from nearly everyone.
Twelve years before the party
At ninety-nine, Bilbo adopts young Frodo as his heir.
S.R. 1401
The eleventy-first birthday. Bilbo vanishes from his own party. The Ring passes to Frodo.
⚡ TRY IT · WHAT DOES THE EVIDENCE SHOW?
Choose the reading the text best supports.
1. The Prologue notes that the Shire has almost no government and that the mayor mostly presides at banquets. What does the detail suggest?
2. In the private scene, Bilbo calls his departure a joke. Which detail shows the joke is not the whole truth?
3. After Bilbo vanishes, Frodo alone says nothing. What does the silence show?
📚 PART 3 · WHY AN ORDINARY HOBBIT

Tolkien could have begun with a war or a throne. He begins with gossip about a rich neighbor and an inheritance, and he describes the finding of the Ring in the Prologue as luck. The narrator also says that in the days of Bilbo and Frodo the hobbits became, by no wish of their own, both important and renowned (Prologue).

That phrase sets the pattern for the book. Importance arrives without being sought, and it arrives in the middle of ordinary life: a party, a will, a label on a parcel. Frodo’s first response to the Ring is that it may be useful. The reader is told more than he is, and the gap between the two is where the dread of the opening chapters comes from.

🔑 KEY TERMS
Shire-reckoning: the hobbits’ calendar, counted from the year they settled the Shire.
heir: the person who receives a legal inheritance, here Bag End and the Ring.
mathom: Tolkien’s word for an object with no present use that its owner will not discard.
narrator: the voice that tells the story; here a historian-like voice that stands outside the hobbits.
