Two hosts
What a welcome is made of
●
Tom Bombadil and Farmer Maggot both take in travellers. A welcome shows in what the host gives, what the host notices, and what the guests carry away.
Tom Bombadil’s house (Chapters 6 to 8)Farmer Maggot’s farm (Chapter 4)
What the host givesA rescue from Old Man Willow, then supper, beds, songs and stories.Beer by the kitchen fire, a large supper with his family, and a ride to the Ferry in his waggon after dark.
What the host noticesWhen Frodo slips the Ring on, the others cannot see him, but Tom can, and he tells Frodo to take it off. The Ring has no hold on Tom.He sees that Frodo is in trouble and does not press him to explain. He has already ordered off a Black Rider who came asking for Baggins.
What the guests carry awayA rhyme to call Tom if danger comes, which Frodo calls on in the barrow.A safe ride to the Ferry, a warning about the Rider, and a basket of mushrooms from Mrs. Maggot.
●
The scene should show a welcome through actions. The note should say what those actions ask of a host, and tie each to the reading.

Quick Write: The Host
Week 3 · 20 points · turned in as this week’s PDF
●
This is the whole Quick Write. Write it in the box at the end of this section. The teacher scores this box.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Write a scene of about 200 words in which a host welcomes a stranger, then a note of about 100 words on what hospitality asks of a host.

●
Focus question. What does a host give a stranger besides food and a bed?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Invent a host and a stranger who arrives tired or afraid. Set the scene in one room.
2
Write the scene of about 200 words. Show the welcome through what the host does and notices: the seat offered, the food, the question held back, the need met.
3
Write the note of about 100 words. Name two things hospitality asks of a host, and tie each to Tom Bombadil’s house or to Farmer Maggot’s farm.
4
Check every pronoun in both parts. Each one needs one clear noun to point back to.
5
Use two of this week’s Word Study words, each used accurately.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
The scene shows the welcome through the host’s actions.
☐
The host notices and meets one need of the stranger.
☐
The note names two things hospitality asks of a host and ties them to the reading.
☐
Every pronoun has one clear noun to point back to.
☐
Two Word Study words are used accurately; the scene runs about 200 words and the note about 100.
HOW IT’S SCORED
Criteria Full credit Partial Pts
The scene
8 pts		 The welcome is shown through the host’s actions, and one need of the stranger is noticed and met. The welcome is told rather than shown, or no need is met. 8
The note on hospitality
8 pts		 Two things hospitality asks of a host are named and tied to Bombadil’s house or Farmer Maggot’s farm. One thing is named, or the tie to the reading is missing. 8
Craft targets
4 pts		 Every pronoun points to one clear noun; two Word Study words used accurately; about 200 and about 100 words. Unclear pronouns, one target missed, or the length is off. 4
Total: 20 pts

5
Who decides who belongs
Hughes and Strider side by side
●
Hughes’s title in the first edition is “Epilogue.” The poem is now better known as “I, Too.”
LANGSTON HUGHES
They send me to eat in the kitchen
When company comes,
Tomorrow,
I’ll sit at the table
When company comes.
Langston Hughes, “Epilogue” (the poem now known as “I, Too”), The Weary Blues (1926; public domain)
Hughes’s speakerStrider at Bree
Who judges?“Company,” the people who send him to the kitchen.The Bree-folk, who know him only as one of the wandering Rangers, and the hobbits, who meet him in a hood.
What is the judgment based on?Appearance and place in the house.His looks and his rough travelling life, until Gandalf’s letter and his conduct count for more.
What answers the judgment?The speaker laughs, eats well, and grows strong. Tomorrow he will sit at the table.Strider warns the hobbits and offers to guide them, and Frodo judges him by how he acts.
●
Both texts show a judgment passed on a person before the person is known. The argument is in what the judgment should rest on, and who has the right to pass it.

Quick Write: A Seat at the Table
Week 4 · 20 points · turned in as this week’s PDF
●
This is the whole Quick Write. Write it in the box at the end of this section. The teacher scores this box.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Write an argument of 250 to 350 words on who decides who belongs, using Langston Hughes’s poem and Strider at Bree.

●
Focus question. Who decides who sits at the table, and how can that judgment be wrong?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
State a claim in the first sentence about who decides who belongs and what the judgment should rest on.
2
Use Hughes. Quote or describe one image from the poem and say whose judgment it shows.
3
Use Strider. Explain how his looks and his wandering life shape what Bree and the hobbits first think, and what changes Frodo’s judgment.
4
Carry the argument with conditions: at least two if or unless clauses that state what follows if belonging rests on appearance, or on conduct.
5
Use two of this week’s Word Study words, each used accurately.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
The first sentence makes a claim about who decides who belongs.
☐
One image from Hughes is explained, not only quoted.
☐
Strider’s case is explained with a specific moment from Chapters 9 and 10.
☐
At least two conditional clauses carry the argument.
☐
Two Word Study words are used accurately, and the writing is 250 to 350 words.
HOW IT’S SCORED
Criteria Full credit Partial Pts
Claim and reasoning
8 pts		 A clear claim about who decides who belongs; the reasoning says what the judgment should rest on and what follows if it rests on appearance. The claim is vague, or the reasoning only restates the two texts. 8
Use of Hughes and Strider
8 pts		 An image from Hughes and a moment from Chapters 9 and 10 are both explained and tied to the claim. One text is missing, or the moments are quoted or retold without explanation. 8
Craft targets
4 pts		 At least two conditional clauses and two Word Study words used accurately; 250 to 350 words. One target is missed, or the length is off. 4
Total: 20 pts
A Seat at the Table: argument (250 to 350 words)
One argument of 250 to 350 words. Check it against the success criteria above before downloading.

Evidence, matched to the format
●
The first four Quick Writes already hold claims and moments that can be reused. Start there, then fill the gaps.
ANALYTICAL OR SYNTHESIS ESSAY
Collect three moments from The Fellowship of the Ring that prove the claim, one moment from another text, and the strongest counterargument. Each moment gets its chapter or part.
DEBATE
Collect two strong moments for each side from The Fellowship of the Ring, and one moment from another text that bears on the question. Each moment gets its chapter or part.
LETTERS OR DIARY
Choose one character and four moments from that character’s life in the book, in order. Add one moment from another text that the afterword will use. Each moment gets its chapter or part.
SHORT FILM OR SCREENPLAY
Choose the one moment the film or screenplay will turn on, and three images or sounds from the book that can carry it. Add one moment from another text. Each gets its chapter or part.
PODCAST OR ORATION
Choose three moments to discuss or read aloud, and one moment from another text. Mark the passage that will be read aloud and keep it short. Each gets its chapter or part.
