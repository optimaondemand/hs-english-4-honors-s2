📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Two words can name the same thing and still change how a reader sees it.
Denotation is what a word means. Connotation is the attitude and association it carries. A writer who picks one word over its near neighbours shapes the reader’s judgment before any argument starts.

📚 PART 1 · SAME FACT, DIFFERENT WORD

A noun or verb has a literal sense and a tone. Lead and herd both describe moving a group, and only herd treats the group like animals. Resolute, stubborn, and obstinate all describe a person who will not yield, and they run from praise to blame. Haldir’s speech and Aragorn’s reply in Chapter 6 show the principle in the setting of this week’s reading.

✎ ON THE BOARD · THREE WAYS DICTION WORKS
The Elves led the Company into the wood.
The Elves herded the Company into the wood.
Same action. Led is neutral and herded suggests that the travellers were treated like animals.
Aragorn on Lothlórien: fair and perilous; but only evil need fear it
Fair and perilous pull in opposite directions. Using both asks the reader to hold admiration and caution together.
Haldir on the Elves: our hands are more often upon the bowstring than upon the harp
Bowstring connotes war and watchfulness. Harp connotes music and peace. The sentence reports a loss without using the word loss.
The guard turned him away.
The guard turned him out.
Away is a refusal at the door. Out implies he had already been let in.
The Dwarf was proud.
The Dwarf was dignified.
Proud can be a fault, and dignified is a compliment. Each is a fair description of the same refusal to walk blindfold.

To read diction, name the literal sense first, then list the nearest alternatives, and ask what the author gained by choosing this word. The effect usually appears in the difference between the chosen word and the second choice.

⚡ TRY IT · CHOOSE THE WORD THAT FITS
Choose the reading the grammar and the context support.
1. A sentence is meant to praise Aragorn for holding the Company together through grief. Which word best fits the sentence: “He was a ____ leader”?
2. Two sentences describe the same blindfold. One says the Elves “covered” the Dwarf’s eyes and the other says they “bound” his eyes. Which word adds a sense of restraint?
3. Haldir contrasts the bowstring with the harp. What is the best reading of the contrast?
📚 PART 2 · WORD STUDY

These six words come from Chapters 6 and 7. Each entry gives the sense the book uses, and the chapter where it appears.

estrangement (noun) · a division between people who should be allies
Root: Latin extraneus, foreign, through Old French estrangier
Appears in: Chapter 6
unscathed (adjective) · not harmed
Root: Old Norse skadi, harm; un- + scathe
Appears in: Chapter 6
irresolute (adjective) · unable to make up one’s mind
Root: Latin resolvere, to loosen or settle, with ir- (not)
Appears in: Chapter 6
poignant (adjective) · keen and vivid to the senses
Root: Old French poindre, to prick, from Latin pungere
Appears in: Chapter 6
obstinate (adjective) · firmly refusing to change course or give way
Root: Latin obstinatus, set on a purpose, from obstinare, to persist
Appears in: Chapter 6
diminish (verb) · to grow less in power, size, or standing
Root: Latin deminuere, to lessen, from de- + minuere, to make smaller
Appears in: Chapter 7
🌐 LOANWORD SPOTLIGHT · BORROWED FROM GREEK

English took this word from Greek, where hybris meant violent insolence; readers of Greek tragedy later used it for the pride that provokes a fall.

hubris (Greek hybris: arrogant pride that ignores limits)
Galadriel’s refusal is the opposite of hubris: she sees the power within reach and declines it.
🔑 KEY TERMS
denotation: the literal meaning of a word.
connotation: the attitude or association a word carries.
diction: an author’s choice of words.
