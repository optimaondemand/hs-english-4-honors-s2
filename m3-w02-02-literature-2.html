Week 2
Clearing the Room
Furniture, memory, and the picture Gregor will not give up
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Grete decides to empty Gregor’s room so that he can crawl freely.
Gregor’s response to the loss shows what he treats as the last evidence of his human life. The lesson reads the argument over the furniture and the act that follows.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can explain the reasons given for and against clearing the room.
2
I can explain why Gregor protects the picture.
3
I can connect objects and memory to a person’s sense of identity.
📋 READING
▶
Reread the stretch of Part II about the clearing of the room, through the mother’s collapse.
📚 PART 1 · A KINDNESS OR AN ERASURE

By now Gregor has taken to crawling over the walls and ceiling, and he leaves traces of his sticky feet behind. Grete decides to make this easier by removing the furniture that gets in his way, starting with the desk and the chest of drawers. She cannot do it alone, so she brings her mother while the father is away.

The mother objects. Bare walls would show the family had given up hope, and leaving the room as it was would let Gregor find everything unchanged if he ever returns to himself. She speaks in a whisper, sure that he cannot follow her words. Gregor, listening, realizes how near he has come to wanting the room emptied. A bare room would let him crawl in every direction, and it would let him forget that he was ever human. It is his mother’s voice, which he has not heard in a long while, that shakes him out of it.

Grete overrules her mother. The narration offers two motives: she has come to see herself as Gregor’s spokeswoman to the parents, and she may be inclined to make his situation seem more shocking than it is so that she can do more for him. She insists on taking out everything except the couch.

⚡ FIRST READ
Gregor hides from Grete but defends the picture against both women. What explains the difference?
📚 PART 2 · WHAT GREGOR PROTECTS

The pieces carry his past. The chest of drawers held his fretsaw and other tools, the hobby that filled his evenings; Gregor had decided he could do without it, and the women have already carried it out. The writing desk, which has left a worn patch in the floor, is where he did his homework from the earliest school years through business training. The desk, he had decided, had to stay.

While the women rest in the next room, leaning on the desk they have now moved there too, Gregor scrambles out, undecided what to save first, and fastens himself onto the framed picture of the woman in furs. He presses his hot belly against the glass. That picture, at least, no one will take. When the women return, Grete’s eyes meet his on the wall. The mother sees a large brown patch against the flowered wallpaper and, before she grasps that it is Gregor, cries out and falls onto the couch. Grete shouts his name, the first word she has spoken to him directly since the change.

Gregor’s stand costs him. His mother collapses, a bottle breaks and cuts his face, caustic medicine splashes over him, and Grete shuts him out. His body has become an animal’s, but what he defends is evidence of a self: a home, a past, and a habit of making things of his own.

⚡ TRY IT · WHAT DOES THE EVIDENCE SHOW?
Choose the reading the text best supports.

🔑 KEY TERMS
identity: the memories, habits, and relationships that make a person who he is.
erasure: the removal of a trace so that something seems never to have been.
