📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Frodo leaves Bag End before he understands the task, and the chapter lets that fact stand.
In Chapter 2 he agreed to keep the Ring and to go. Chapter 3 shows what the agreement costs in practice: a house sold, a cover story told, a destination he does not have, and a guide who does not arrive.

📚 PART 1 · SELLING BAG END

Gandalf tells Frodo to go quietly and soon. Frodo delays for weeks. He wants one more summer in the Shire, and he fixes his departure for his fiftieth birthday, the day that fits best with following Bilbo. Nearly all of the preparation is a matter of concealment. He sells Bag End to the Sackville-Bagginses, buys a small house at Crickhollow in Buckland with Merry’s help, and lets the district believe that his money is running out and that he is moving back to his mother’s people. Of his friends, only Sam is told the real plan.

The cover story works because it uses the Shire’s own gossip. Hobbiton already believes that Bag End is full of treasure and that Gandalf is behind every oddity, so a sale at a bargain price is easy to explain in either direction. The Shire, which sees only what it expects, supplies its own camouflage.

When Frodo asks what he is supposed to do, his words mark the difference from Bilbo’s journey:

Bilbo went to find a treasure, there and back again; but I go to lose one, and not return, as far as I can see. (Book I, Chapter 3)

Gandalf does not correct the picture. He answers that Frodo cannot see very far (Book I, Chapter 3) and that neither can he. The wizard offers a direction, toward Rivendell and toward danger but not too rashly, and not a plan. Then he leaves the Shire to gather news and fails to return in time. Frodo sets out on foot, at night, with Pippin and Sam, and gives the key to Sam at the door.

⚡ FIRST READ
Frodo says Bilbo went to find a treasure and that he goes to lose one. What is the difference in kind between the two journeys?
⚖ TWO DEPARTURES, SIDE BY SIDE
Bilbo, Chapter 1Frodo, Chapter 3
How he leavesBy a party, a speech, and a vanishing trick before a hundred guestsBy a sale, a cover story, and a walk at night through back fields
Who goes with himThree dwarves, packed and waitingTwo hobbit friends, only one of whom knows the real purpose
What he is afterMountains, rest, and a place to finish his bookSafety for the Shire; a road with no clear end
His moodDelighted to be off, sure that it is funReluctant, wishing the departure were not necessary
The song on the roadHe sings of the Road and of following it with eager feetFrodo says nearly the same verse, with weary feet

The last row of the table repays a second look. The verse about the Road returns in Chapter 3 with one word changed. In Chapter 1 Bilbo sings it as he steps into the dark, and the word is eager. In Chapter 3 Frodo says it to himself on the hill above the Woody End, and the word is weary. Frodo is not sure whether the verse is a memory or a thing he is making up, and Pippin finds it discouraging. A single adjective carries the difference between a holiday and a task.

Frodo’s memory of Bilbo’s advice about the Road adds to this. Bilbo used to say that the Road is like a river with its springs at every doorstep, and that a hobbit who steps onto it without keeping his feet may be carried anywhere. The picture suits Frodo’s situation: he has stepped in, and he does not control where it leads.

⚡ TRY IT · WHAT DOES THE EVIDENCE SHOW?
Choose the reading the text best supports.
1. The verse about the Road appears in Chapter 1 with “eager feet” and in Chapter 3 with “weary feet”. What does the change signal?
2. Gandalf tells Frodo that neither of them can see very far. What does the admission do?
3. Gildor tells the hobbits they can fence themselves in but cannot fence the world out for ever. How does this connect with the earlier lesson on the cave?
📚 PART 2 · A CALL ACCEPTED BEFORE IT IS UNDERSTOOD

Frodo is not the only one who sets out before understanding. When Frodo tells Sam in Chapter 4 that the journey may be a one-way trip, Sam says that after the night with the Elves he feels something different. He does not know what he wants, he says, but I have something to do before the end, and it lies ahead, not in the Shire. (Book I, Chapter 4) This is not a reason Sam could have given a week before. It is a pull he cannot name and does not question.

The Elves do not dispel the uncertainty. Gildor admits he does not know why the Enemy pursues Frodo, and he says the choice to go or to wait is Frodo’s. When Frodo asks where he will find courage, Gildor answers briefly: Courage is found in unlikely places (Book I, Chapter 3). The answer neither flatters Frodo nor promises that he has the thing he needs.

The pattern is the one the module will follow. A call comes to ordinary people, and it comes without a clear aim, a map, or a promise. What the chapter shows in answer is a series of small acts: a key handed over, a gate closed, a farewell to dark windows. The call is accepted one step at a time, before it is understood.

🔑 KEY TERMS
cover story: a false account given to hide a real purpose.
quest: a journey with a purpose; Bilbo’s was for a treasure, Frodo’s is to be rid of one.
counsel: advice offered by someone who leaves the decision to the person advised.
duty: a task a person is bound to by role, promise or conscience, whether or not it was sought.
