The inn gives the hobbits a hot meal, a parlour built for them, and a crowded room.
The same room carries the news that spreads. Welcome and exposure arrive together, and the evening ends with Frodo disappearing in front of strangers.

📋 READING
▶
Read Chapter 9 of Book I, At the Sign of the Prancing Pony, in your copy.
💡 THE SETTING

Bree is the main village of the Bree-land, a small district of four villages outside the Shire. Men and hobbits live there side by side and regard one another as necessary parts of the community. The East Road crosses an older road just outside the village, and the inn stands on the Road.

Shire hobbits call the hobbits of Bree, and any others beyond the border, Outsiders, and take little interest in them. The village gates are closed at nightfall.

📚 PART 1 · A WELCOME WITH CONDITIONS

The four hobbits arrive after dark. The gatekeeper at the West-gate questions them before he lets them in, and when Frodo says their names and business are their own, the gatekeeper answers briefly: it’s my business to ask questions after nightfall.

Inside, the welcome is warm. The innkeeper, Barliman Butterbur, is overworked and forgetful, but he has rooms made for hobbits on the ground floor, with round windows, and he sends supper to a private parlour. Frodo gives the name Underhill. Several Underhills of Staddle take this as proof of kinship and treat him as a long-lost cousin. Sam, for his part, is alarmed by the size of the inn and pictures Black Riders at its upper windows.

Two details complicate the welcome. As soon as the gatekeeper turns his back, a dark figure climbs over the gate and slips into the village. And Butterbur’s invitation to join the company in the common-room comes with a plain request: the inn likes news, stories and songs from the Shire.

⚡ FIRST READ
Which description of Butterbur’s welcome best fits the arrival scenes?
📚 PART 2 · THE COMMON-ROOM

The common-room holds Men of Bree, local hobbits, dwarves, and travelers from the South who came up the Greenway. The talk turns to trouble in the South. The Bree-folk are sympathetic but plainly unwilling to take in large numbers of strangers. The hobbits pay little attention, since the matter does not seem to concern hobbits.

Pippin, enjoying the attention, begins a comic account of Bilbo’s farewell party and heads for the disappearance. Frodo sees that this will tie the name Baggins to the Ring. A hooded stranger by the wall, who is later called Strider, tells him to act: You had better do something quick!

Frodo climbs on a table and makes a short speech, and while he speaks he feels a wish to put on the Ring and vanish. The wish seems to him to arise outside himself, as though some presence in the room were prompting it. He resists it, then sings one of Bilbo’s songs about an inn. During the encore he falls from the table and disappears. Afterward he supposes that the Ring slid onto his finger as he flung out his hand to save his fall, and he wonders whether the Ring itself played a trick.

The mood turns at once. Most of the local hobbits and Men leave, some with dark looks, and Pippin and Sam are left alone in a corner, regarded as companions of a traveling magician. Two men who had whispered together all evening, a swarthy Bree-lander and the squint-eyed southerner, slip out.

📚 PART 3 · WHAT THE HOST RISKS

Butterbur is not much put out. He expects the mystery to fill his house on later nights, but he warns Frodo that his customers dislike anything out of the ordinary: We’re a bit suspicious round here of anything out of the way

The scene shows a host weighing two things at once: the custom a lively evening brings, and the unease a strange guest raises. Frodo, shaken, begins to suspect that everyone in the inn is in league against him, and he even doubts Butterbur’s round face. Whether the suspicion is fair is left to the reader, and the next chapter supplies evidence.

🔑 KEY TERMS
hospitality: the welcome and protection a host gives a guest.
alias: a name used in place of one’s own.
host: the person who receives guests and answers for their comfort and for what happens in the house.
