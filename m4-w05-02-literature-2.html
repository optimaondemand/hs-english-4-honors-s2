Week 5
The Council
Each people comes with its own errand, and the Ring is everyone’s problem.
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Elrond opens the Council by saying the company was called though no one summoned it.
Each speaker then lays out one people’s stake. The chapter builds a picture in which no single people can claim the Ring or carry it.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can state what each speaker brings to the Council.
2
I can explain Elrond’s statement that the company was “so ordered” and what it implies.
3
I can explain why the proposals to hide, wield or destroy the Ring are weighed as they are.
📋 READING
▶
Read Chapter 2 of Book II, The Council of Elrond, in your copy, up to the point where Frodo speaks.
📚 PART 1 · WHO COMES, AND WHY

Elrond introduces Frodo to the gathering and says that he has come through great peril. Glóin then reports that a messenger from Mordor has offered the dwarves rings and the realm of Moria in return for news of a hobbit and his small ring. Elrond answers him: your trouble is but part of the trouble of all the western world.

Elrond then explains that the company met by no one’s summons: Called, I say, though I have not called you to me. He adds that it only seems chance: it is so ordered that we, who sit here, and none others, must now find counsel for the peril of the world. The statement places the duty above any single host or guest. No one commanded the Council, yet its members are bound to answer.

⚡ FIRST READ
Elrond says no one summoned the company, yet its members are bound to find counsel. Which reading fits?
📚 PART 2 · THE TALE OF THE RING

Elrond tells the history of the Ring: Sauron’s forging of it, the Last Alliance, and Isildur’s choice to keep it instead of destroying it. Boromir had not heard that tale. He speaks for Gondor, a bulwark that has held the passages of the river while the rest of the West was kept at peace, and he came for counsel, not allies.

Aragorn answers Boromir’s claim with another kind of service, the secret watch of the Rangers: If simple folk are free from care and fear, simple they will be, and we must be secret to keep them so. The speech recasts Strider’s treatment at Bree. The people who keep the peace are not thanked because the peace hides them.

Each speaker, in short, sees the Ring through his own people’s need. Boromir’s need is a weapon. Glóin’s is a bargain, and the Elves’ is survival. Elrond and Gandalf weigh the whole.

📚 PART 3 · WHAT TO DO WITH IT

The Council tests four plans. Bombadil could keep the Ring, but Gandalf says it has no power over him and he is his own master, and that he would be an unsafe guardian who would soon forget or discard it. Sending it over the Sea fails because those beyond would not receive it, and the deeps are not safe forever. Elrond then names the one road left: We must send the Ring to the Fire. Boromir then proposes using it as a weapon instead, and Elrond refuses: its strength is too great for anyone who does not already have great power, and for them the desire of it corrupts the heart. He will not take it to wield, and Gandalf says the same.

Erestor calls that path despair, or folly. Gandalf replies that despair belongs to those who see the end beyond doubt, and that It is wisdom to recognize necessity. Because the Enemy judges every heart by desire for power, he will not expect anyone to try to destroy the Ring.

⚡ TRY IT
Choose the reading the chapter best supports.
1. Boromir proposes using the Ring as a weapon. Which response best captures Elrond’s answer?
2. Why does Gandalf say the plan to destroy the Ring may succeed where strength would fail?
🔑 KEY TERMS
bulwark: a wall or defence against attack; here, Gondor’s role as the West’s shield.
necessity: something that must be done because no other course remains.
