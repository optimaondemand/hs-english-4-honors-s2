1
What this is
One world, one spring

This spring you enter one world from a book you read this year, take a public role there, and spend the semester moving a real audience toward a goal you can defend as wise. Every lesson page is where the work happens: you read, choose, and write right there on the page. At the end of each lesson you download a PDF and upload it to that lesson's assignment in Canvas.

2
The one rule
Who you are actually talking to

Your teacher is never the audience of anything you make. You write and speak to the people you chose: a council, a jury, a congregation of readers, a public that could actually be moved. Your teacher reads your work the way one member of that audience would, then reads the rest of what you turn in.

Your five media
One reaches your audience better than the others
Vlog
Three short episodes, on camera, in role.
Podcast
Two long episodes, or one longer one, in role.
Feature article
One long-form piece, published as its own web page.
Imitation
A public genre, written or performed, from your world or ours.
The Address
A formal spoken case, delivered live to a gathered audience.

See the medium menu for what each one asks of you and where it meets a public.

5
What you keep as you go
Six working files, one folder

Keep these six instruments current in your English IV Honors folder in OneDrive. Name every file LastName_ENG4H_<Item>.

  • Your sources list: ITW_Sources.docx
  • Your look-and-sound board: ITW_LookAndSoundBoard.docx
  • Your permissions log: ITW_PermissionsLog.docx
  • Your AI log: ITW_AILog.docx
  • Your letter: ITW_InquiryLetter.docx
  • Your accuracy note: ITW_AccuracyNote.docx
6
Claude, plainly
What it can do, and what it never does

Claude can check a fact or an anachronism, play your audience and object to you, tidy a transcript, and build a web page from text you have already finished. It never writes the words your audience reads or hears. Every use goes in your AI log, the same day you use it.

7
Two books in copyright
The Metamorphosis and The Fellowship of the Ring

Both spring books are still in copyright. Read your own approved copy. Quote no more than a short phrase for analysis. Do not reproduce the text or any maps, and do not recreate a scene. Work set in Middle-earth is published to the class gallery discussion in Canvas only and never shared outside the course.
