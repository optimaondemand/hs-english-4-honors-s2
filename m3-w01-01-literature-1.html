📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
The story opens with a transformation, reported plainly.
Gregor Samsa wakes as a giant insect-like creature, and his first thoughts go to his job, not his body. The lesson reads how the plain telling produces the story’s strangeness and opens the question of whether Gregor is still human.

📚 HISTORICAL CONTEXT
Franz Kafka (1883-1924) was born in Prague, then part of the Austro-Hungarian Empire, and wrote in German. He worked days at an insurance office and wrote in the evenings. The Metamorphosis (German title: Die Verwandlung) was published in 1915. Kafka asked his publisher not to put a picture of the insect on the cover. The English word Kafkaesque describes a situation that is absurd, impersonal, and beyond a person’s control.
📚 PART 1 · A STRANGE FACT, PLAINLY STATED

The first sentence reports the change as a completed fact. Gregor is already a creature with a domed brown belly, hard plates along the back, and many thin legs, and the narration lists these details with the exactness of an inventory. No cause is offered, and none is promised later.

The room around him is ordinary: a bed, a table spread with cloth samples, a magazine picture in a frame on the wall. Gregor travels for a living, and the room belongs to a working man who lives with his family. The contrast between an unexplained event and an exact, domestic setting is the main source of the story’s effect. Horror fiction usually explains itself. This story withholds the explanation and carries on with the morning.

📚 PART 2 · THE WRONG THING TO WORRY ABOUT

After a first look at his body, Gregor’s thoughts turn to the working day he is about to miss. He tries to roll onto his right side to sleep a little longer and cannot. He complains about the traveling career: early trains, poor meals, and acquaintances who never become friends. He counts what the missed train will cost. The office assistant will report it, the boss will suspect a pretext, and the doctor from the health insurer will treat any illness as laziness.

He also puts off the thought of quitting. His parents owe a debt to the boss, and by his own estimate five or six more years of work will clear it. He explains his altered voice as a cold, the kind of illness his trade makes common, and he plans to catch the next train.

Read closely, these thoughts show a life organized around being useful. Gregor does not ask what has happened to him. He asks how to get to work. The narration stays inside his view, so the reader learns how little room his own body and wishes had taken up in his thinking before the change. Whether a mind that worries in this way still makes him human, in this form, is the open question of the module.

📚 PART 3 · A WORD TRANSLATORS DISAGREE ABOUT

Kafka’s German word for what Gregor has become is Ungeziefer. It originally meant an unclean animal, one unfit to be offered in sacrifice, and it names no particular species. English translators have chosen differently: vermin, insect, bug, and others. A narrow word such as beetle makes the creature one specific animal. A broad word keeps it undefined, which is closer to Kafka’s practice of leaving the image unfixed. His request about the cover points the same way: the creature is not to be shown.

Check which word the assigned edition uses and what it suggests: a pest, an animal, or something less defined.

⏱ THE ORDER OF GREGOR'S CONCERNS BEFORE ANYONE SEES HIM
1
His body
Sees the belly and legs; asks what has happened, and registers that it is not a dream.
2
Sleep
Tries to turn onto his right side to sleep longer; fails, and gives up the attempt.
3
Work
Complains about the traveling job, the debt, and the five o’clock train.
4
The clock
Finds it past half past six; weighs the seven o’clock train and the risk of reporting sick.
5
His voice
Answers his mother in a changed voice and calls it a cold.
⚡ TRY IT · WHAT DOES THE EVIDENCE SHOW?
Choose the reading the text best supports.
1. The narration reports the transformation in a flat, factual way and gives no cause. What effect does this produce?
2. Gregor lists complaints about his job before he tries to leave the bed. What does the order suggest?
3. Translators render Ungeziefer as vermin, insect, or bug. Why does the choice matter?
🔑 TAKEAWAY

The opening withholds explanation, reports the change in a level tone, and shows Gregor’s attention fixed on work. The question it raises is how much of a person is the body and how much is mind and role.
