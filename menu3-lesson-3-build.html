Trust Thyself?
Quick Write · Week 5 · 20 points
●
This is the whole Quick Write. Write it in the boxes at the end of this section. Your teacher scores it with the table below.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Test one claim from Emerson’s “Self-Reliance” against Gregor in The Metamorphosis or against Miss Brill. Write 300 to 400 words: the claim, the evidence, the counterpoint.

●
Focus question. Does the self that answers only to itself find strength, or find itself alone?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Choose one Emerson claim from the list in this tab or from this week’s excerpts, and state it in your own words.
2
Choose Gregor or Miss Brill and apply the claim to one specific moment. Say what the claim explains.
3
Give the counterpoint: where the claim fails the character, or what trusting the self costs.
4
End with a judgment: does the character show the strength or the risk of trusting the self?
5
Use one antithesis sentence (two opposed ideas in balanced structure) and at least two Word Study words, used accurately.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
One Emerson claim is stated accurately in your own words.
☐
The claim is applied to one specific moment in the story.
☐
A counterpoint is stated and weighed.
☐
The last sentence gives a judgment.
☐
One antithesis sentence and two Word Study words are used well, and the writing is 300 to 400 words.
HOW IT’S SCORED
CriteriaFull creditPartialPts
Claim and application
8 pts		Emerson’s claim is stated accurately and applied to a specific moment so that it explains something about the character.The claim is paraphrased loosely, or the application stays general.8
Evidence and counterpoint
8 pts		Accurate evidence from the story, a counterpoint that takes the claim seriously, and a judgment that follows from both.Thin evidence, a counterpoint that is brushed aside, or no judgment.8
Craft targets
4 pts		One antithesis sentence and two Word Study words used accurately; 300 to 400 words.Fewer than two of these, or off the length target.4
Total:20 pts

5
Marcus Answers Emerson
Quick Write · Week 6 · 20 points
●
This is the whole Quick Write. Write it in the boxes at the end of this section. Your teacher scores it with the table below.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Write a letter of 250 to 350 words in Marcus Aurelius’s voice, answering one line of Emerson’s “Self-Reliance.”

●
Focus question. What would a man who sees himself as one part of a whole say to the thinker who tells him to trust himself alone?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Choose one Emerson line from the tab or from last week’s excerpts.
2
Decide what Marcus would grant that line and what he would refuse. Draw on what is in our power, the fellow-workers passage, or acceptance.
3
Write in Marcus’s voice: calm, direct, addressed to Emerson.
4
Use at least two imperative sentences, as Marcus gives commands to himself.
5
Include one phrase from the Meditations excerpts, in quotation marks.
6
Use at least two of this week’s Word Study words, used accurately.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
One Emerson line is named and understood.
☐
The letter says what Marcus would grant and what he would refuse.
☐
The voice is steady and consistent throughout.
☐
Two imperative sentences and one phrase from Marcus are included.
☐
At least two Word Study words are used accurately, and the letter is 250 to 350 words.
HOW IT’S SCORED
CriteriaFull creditPartialPts
Understanding of both thinkers
8 pts		Emerson’s line is read accurately, and Marcus’s answer follows from ideas in the Meditations.One thinker is misread, or the answer could belong to anyone.8
Voice and use of Marcus
8 pts		A consistent, convincing voice, with an apt phrase from Marcus worked into the argument.The voice slips into modern speech, or the phrase is dropped in without purpose.8
Craft targets
4 pts		Two imperative sentences and two Word Study words used accurately; 250 to 350 words.Fewer than two of these, or off the length target.4
Total:20 pts
Your Quick Write
A letter of 250 to 350 words in Marcus’s voice.

7
Build the core
By format
●
The core is the part of the project that carries the claim. Build it now, before the draft, using what Emerson, Marcus, and Herbert add to the argument.
BUILD THE CORE: ANALYTICAL OR SYNTHESIS ESSAY
Write the body. Each section opens with a claim, brings one text moment in with frame, quote, explain, and ends by saying what the moment shows. Write the counterargument and its answer.
BUILD THE CORE: DEBATE
Write both sides at full strength, each with its best evidence from at least two texts. Write the judgment last, and say which side it favors and why.
BUILD THE CORE: LETTERS OR DIARY
Write every letter or entry in its writer’s voice. Tie each to a text moment, and let the sequence show an interpretation. Draft the afterword last.
BUILD THE CORE: SHORT FILM OR SCREENPLAY
Write the scenes or script pages, or shoot the footage. Tie each choice of framing, sound, or dialogue to a text moment and to the claim.
BUILD THE CORE: PODCAST OR ORATION
Write the script for every segment, and mark the passage you will read aloud. Record a first pass of the opening to hear the pace.

Week 7 · The Call in “The Collar”
●
The poem opens in revolt and ends in a call. Read the first two lines and the last four together.
I struck the board, and cry’d, No more;
I will abroad.
But as I rav’d and grew more fierce and wilde
At every word,
Me thoughts I heard one calling, Childe:
And I reply’d, My Lord.
Herbert, “The Collar”, The Temple (1633; public domain)
9
The Call in “The Collar”
Quick Write · Week 7 · 20 points
●
This is the whole Quick Write. Write it in the boxes at the end of this section. Your teacher scores it with the table below.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Explain the turn in the last four lines of “The Collar” and what the speaker’s answer accepts. Write 250 to 350 words.

●
Focus question. What changes between the speaker’s revolt and his answer, and what does he accept without giving up his voice?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
In one sentence, say what changes between the speaker’s opening revolt and the last four lines.
2
Explain how the voice that calls “Childe” differs from the speaker’s own voice. “My Lord” names the speaker’s Christian God.
3
Say what the reply accepts, and what the speaker does not give up.
4
Name one device (apostrophe, personification, or extended metaphor) in the poem and say what it does.
5
Compare the speaker with Emerson’s self-reliant person in one or two sentences.
6
Use at least two of this week’s Word Study words, used accurately.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
The turn in the last four lines is located and described accurately.
☐
The two voices are distinguished.
☐
The reply is explained: what it accepts and what it keeps.
☐
One device is named and its effect explained, and the Emerson comparison is made.
☐
At least two Word Study words are used accurately, and the writing is 250 to 350 words.
HOW IT’S SCORED
CriteriaFull creditPartialPts
The turn
8 pts		Locates the turn exactly, separates the two voices, and explains what changes.Says the speaker “gives in” without showing how the lines make that happen.8
What the answer accepts
8 pts		Explains what the reply accepts and what the speaker keeps, with evidence from the poem and a comparison to Emerson.Treats the reply as plain surrender or plain faith without evidence.8
Craft targets
4 pts		One device named and its effect explained; two Word Study words used accurately; 250 to 350 words.Fewer than two of these, or off the length target.4
Total:20 pts
Your Quick Write
250 to 350 words.
