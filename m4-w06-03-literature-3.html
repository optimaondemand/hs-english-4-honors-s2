Week 6
The Bridge
Gandalf’s stand at the Bridge of Khazad-dûm, and what separates sacrifice from self-destruction.
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
At the Bridge one member holds the narrow way so that the others can pass.
The chapter lets a reader ask what makes this a sacrifice and not a loss. Gregor Samsa’s ending raises the same question from the other side.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can describe how the chapter prepares the Bridge scene with the record of Balin’s colony.
2
I can explain what Gandalf does at the Bridge and what the others do.
3
I can compare Gandalf’s fall with Gregor’s death on the points that separate sacrifice from self-destruction.
📋 READING
▶
Read Chapter 5 of Book II, The Bridge of Khazad-dûm, in your copy.
📚 PART 1 · THE RECORD OF THE DEAD

The Company finds the tomb of Balin and a broken book that records his colony’s end. The last pages say that the dwarves had barred the gates and could not leave. One entry repeats: We cannot get out. Gandalf judges the attempt to retake Moria valiant but foolish. The colony stands as a warning of what this road can do to those who take it.

Then drums sound in the depths and orcs attack the chamber. Frodo, in a sudden fury, stabs at the foot of a troll. An orc-chieftain’s spear pins him to the wall, and Aragorn carries him out thinking him dead. To everyone’s surprise Frodo is alive, bruised but whole. Gandalf stays behind to shut the door and returns exhausted, saying that he has met his match.

⚡ FIRST READ
Gandalf tells the Company to run and says he must hold the narrow way. What does the order show?
📚 PART 2 · THE NARROW WAY

The Company runs through a hall lit by fire to a narrow stone bridge without rails. The Balrog appears behind the orcs and leaps the fissure. Gandalf halts at the brink and the others gather behind him. His order is exact: Fly! This is a foe beyond any of you. I must hold the narrow way. He takes the middle of the span and says repeatedly, You cannot pass.

When the Balrog advances, Aragorn and Boromir run back to stand beside him, and Aragorn cries, He cannot stand alone! But Gandalf breaks the bridge under the Balrog with his staff. The Balrog falls, and its whip drags Gandalf over the edge. His last words are an order to the others: Fly, you fools! Aragorn then leads them out, and the Company weeps in the open air.

The details show a stand taken for a purpose. Gandalf has named the foe, given his order, chosen his ground, and used the one move that could end the pursuit. The others share the call, since they run back to stand with him even when told to flee.

📚 PART 3 · SACRIFICE OR SELF-DESTRUCTION?

Elrond had said earlier that the road would be Gandalf’s great work, and maybe the end of his labours. The fall at the Bridge can be read as that end. Four marks help to tell sacrifice from self-destruction: the act is chosen, it serves a purpose beyond the one who acts, the cost is known, and others can name what was given.

Gregor Samsa’s death, in Part III of Kafka’s story, can be set beside it, and the story supports more than one reading. Near the end Gregor thinks of his family with tenderness and holds that he must disappear. The family, after his death, is relieved and goes on an outing in the open air to speak of its prospects. The reading that treats this as a gift stresses his love and the family’s freedom. The reading that treats it as erasure stresses that Grete had already insisted the family must be rid of him, and that the household’s quick relief shows how little it believed it had lost. Both readings can be held open.

⚖ TWO ENDINGS
Gandalf at the BridgeGregor at the end
Was the act chosen?Yes: he names the foe, orders the others to flee, and takes the spanUnclear: he withdraws and fades, and the story gives no clear act
Who benefits?The Company, which escapes and goes on with the RingThe family, who are freed from care of him
Was the cost known?Yes: he calls the foe beyond any of themHe seems to think he must go, but the story does not say he understands what it costs
How is the loss met?With grief: the Company weeps as it leavesWith relief: the family turns to the future
⚡ TRY IT
Choose the reading the texts best support.
1. Which feature most clearly separates Gandalf’s fall from Gregor’s death?
2. Aragorn and Boromir run back to stand with Gandalf. What does the moment show?
🔑 KEY TERMS
sacrifice: the giving up of something valued for the sake of a purpose or another person.
self-destruction: the loss of oneself without a purpose beyond the loss.
