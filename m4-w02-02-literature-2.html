📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
The Black Riders follow a scent and a name, and neither is Frodo.
In Chapters 3 to 5 the pursuers never address Frodo as a person. They search for the Ring and for the name tied to it, and the hobbit who carries it is an obstacle or a location. The pattern invites a comparison with the way Gregor is treated in The Metamorphosis.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can describe each Black Rider encounter in Chapters 3 to 5 with evidence.
2
I can explain how the Riders’ behavior treats Frodo as a thing to be found.
3
I can compare Frodo’s situation with Gregor’s, noting what the two share and where they differ.
📋 READING
▶
Reread the Rider scenes in Book I, Chapters 3 to 5, in your copy: on the road in Chapter 3, at the farm in Chapter 4, and at the ferry in Chapter 5.
📚 PART 1 · THREE ENCOUNTERS, ONE PATTERN

The first Rider comes along the road behind the hobbits in Chapter 3. He wears black, sits low in the saddle, and carries his face in shadow. He stops level with the tree where Frodo is hiding, and the narrator reports a sound like someone sniffing for a scent. Frodo feels sure the Rider is looking or smelling for him. He is also surprised by an urge to put on the Ring, strong enough that he moves a hand toward it.

At night in Chapter 3 a second encounter is worse. A dark shape leads a horse along the lane, bends to the ground, and crawls toward Frodo, sniffing. Again the urge to use the Ring comes, stronger than before. Elvish voices sound in the wood, and the shape retreats.

In Chapter 4 Farmer Maggot describes the Rider who came to his door. The Rider asked, in a queer voice, whether Maggot had seen Baggins, then whispered that Baggins was coming and was not far off, and offered to return with gold for news. Maggot’s dog Grip fled from him, howling. In Chapter 5 Sam points out a figure on the far landing-stage that sways and searches the ground, then crawls out of the lamplight.

⚡ FIRST READ
What does the Rider seem to be looking for in Chapter 3?
📚 PART 2 · WHAT THE RIDERS WANT

Three details put the pattern together. First, the Riders use their noses before their eyes. The narrator reports a sniffing sound at the first meeting and snuffling at the second, and Maggot’s dog yelps and flees. Second, the Rider speaks of Frodo by his family name and speaks of him in the third person: Baggins has left, Baggins is coming. Third, he offers gold for news, so that Frodo becomes something with a price.

The effect on the reader is a kind of vertigo. Frodo is the center of the pursuit and is never the object of its attention. He is a bearer. The Riders do not want what he thinks, feels, or has done. They want what he has, and his whole self is reduced to the pocket in which it sits.

The Ring itself makes the reduction worse. Each time a Rider nears, Frodo feels a pull to wear the Ring, and he notes that Gandalf’s warning seems absurd. The thing that makes him a target also works on him to give himself away. In Gandalf’s earlier account, a wearer who uses the Ring often fades and ends by walking permanently unseen. Even the apparent escape, vanishing, would be a further step in the same direction: being seen only as what he carries, and then not being seen at all.

🔍 HOW EACH PARTY TREATS FRODO
THE RIDERS
Look for: a scent and a name.
Address him as: “Baggins”, in the third person.
Offer: gold for news of where he is.
Effect: Frodo is a location, not a person.
GILDOR AND THE ELVES
Look for: a traveler in the dark.
Address him as: Frodo, by his own name, and later as an Elf-friend.
Offer: shelter, counsel, and a blessing.
Effect: Frodo is a guest.
MERRY, PIPPIN AND SAM
Look for: a friend who is about to go alone.
Address him as: “dear old Frodo”.
Offer: company on the road.
Effect: Frodo is someone known.
⚡ TRY IT · WHAT DOES THE EVIDENCE SHOW?
Choose the reading the text best supports.
1. At Maggot’s gate the Rider asks for Baggins and offers gold for news. What does this show about how he sees Frodo?
2. Each time a Rider draws near, Frodo feels the urge to put on the Ring. Which reading does the text best support?
3. How is the Riders’ view of Frodo like the Samsa household’s view of Gregor?
📚 PART 3 · A LOOK BACK AT GREGOR

In Part III of The Metamorphosis Grete begins to speak of her brother as “it”, and the change is explained by what he now costs the household and not by what he is. The chief clerk in Part I had already treated him as a worker whose absence is a fault. Gregor is seen, in turn, as an employee, a burden, and a nuisance, and each view leaves out the person.

The comparison has two edges. The resemblance is that both Gregor and Frodo are valued by others for a function: Gregor for his wages, Frodo for the Ring. The difference lies in who does the reducing. The people who reduce Gregor are the people who made him what he was, his family and his firm. The people who reduce Frodo are strangers and servants of an enemy, and the people close to him do the opposite. The next lesson turns to them.

🔑 KEY TERMS
bearer: the person who carries something of value or danger, here the Ring.
pursuit: the act of following someone in order to catch him.
reduction: treating a person as one aspect of himself, such as his usefulness or what he owns.
scent: a smell used to track; the Riders rely on it before sight.
