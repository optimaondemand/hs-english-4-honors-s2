📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
The Council ends when Frodo speaks.
Elrond will not lay the task on him, and Sam will not be left. Two other offers made at the Council, Bilbo’s and Boromir’s, help to show why the task cannot be seized and cannot be forced. The module’s central question turns on these pages.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can explain why the chapter shows the Ring as everyone’s problem and no one people’s responsibility.
2
I can explain why Elrond calls the task appointed and yet refuses to lay it on anyone.
3
I can compare Frodo’s offer with the offers of Bilbo and Boromir.
📋 READING
▶
Reread the end of Chapter 2 of Book II, The Council of Elrond, from Boromir’s proposal to the close of the chapter, in your copy.
📚 PART 1 · THE OFFER

After the long silence, Frodo speaks with the sense that another will is using his small voice: I will take the Ring ... though I do not know the way. The offer is short and has no plan behind it. Elrond answers that the task seems appointed for Frodo, and then adds a limit to his own authority: It is a heavy burden. So heavy that none could lay it on another. I do not lay it on you.

The two statements fit together. A call can be real without being forced. Elrond will say that the choice is right only if it is made freely: if you take it freely, I will say that your choice is right. The task is Frodo’s to answer, not Elrond’s to impose.

⚡ FIRST READ
Elrond says the task is appointed for Frodo and also that he does not lay it on him. How do these fit?
📚 PART 2 · OTHER HANDS AT THE RING

Sam, who had been sitting quietly on the floor, cries out that Frodo cannot be sent off alone. Elrond smiles and sets him beside Frodo: You at least shall go with him. Sam was not summoned to the Council and is now part of its answer. His place rests on loyalty to a person more than on any task or office.

Two other offers frame Frodo’s. Boromir proposes using the Ring as a weapon in Gondor’s defence, and Elrond refuses, but Boromir accepts the ruling with doubt, bowing his head. Bilbo offers to finish what he started. Gandalf declines, and says that starting is too great a claim for any, and that the old hobbit’s part now is to write the record.

Aragorn adds another limit on claims. When Frodo thinks the Ring should belong to Isildur’s heir, Aragorn replies: It does not belong to either of us ... but it has been ordained that you should hold it for a while. No one at the Council owns the Ring.

📚 PART 3 · EVERYONE’S PROBLEM, NO ONE’S TO CARRY

The Council’s pattern is now clear. Each people has a reason to care about the Ring, and no people can be its owner. Gondor has valour, the Dwarves a bargain, the Elves a memory, and the wizards a plan. The Ring is everyone’s problem. It is no one people’s responsibility. A task of that kind is not met by private preference alone, and it is not met by loyalty to one’s own people alone. It calls for character and purpose beyond both.

Elrond sums up the odd shape of such work in a sentence about who ends up doing it: small hands do them because they must, while the eyes of the great are elsewhere. The line explains Frodo and Sam. It does not explain a command, because no one gives one. A call of this kind arrives, and each person answers it from some mix of purpose, loyalty and a sense of what is right. The next chapter shows the Company that results.

⚡ TRY IT
Choose the reading the chapter best supports.
1. Why does the Council treat the Ring as no one people’s responsibility?
2. Sam is added to the task although he was not summoned. What does his case add?
🔑 KEY TERMS
appointed: assigned to a particular person, though not necessarily by command.
burden: a heavy load, whether of duty, grief or responsibility.
call: a summons to duty that is answered by choice, not enforced by authority.
