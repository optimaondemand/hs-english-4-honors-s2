Week 6
The Ring Goes South
The Company is named, bound by no oath, and turned back by the mountain.
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Elrond names the Company and gives it a charge that no one is bound to keep.
Each member comes by a different road: purpose, loyalty to a person or a people, and a call to what is right. The Company then leaves Rivendell in the dark, and Caradhras forces it back.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can describe the different roads by which the members of the Company come to it.
2
I can explain why Elrond lays no oath or bond on the Company, and what Gimli’s reply adds.
3
I can explain how the pass at Caradhras tests the Company’s plan, and who reads the storm in which way.
📋 READING
▶
Read Chapter 3 of Book II, The Ring Goes South, in your copy.
📚 PART 1 · THE COMPANY IS NAMED

Merry and Pippin are indignant that Sam crept into the Council and was chosen. Gandalf warns them that nothing is decided, and the scouts Elrond sends out take two months to return with no sign of the Black Riders. Elrond then chooses the Company: the Nine Walkers shall be set against the Nine Riders that are evil. Gandalf goes, as does Sam. Legolas goes for the Elves, Gimli for the Dwarves, and Aragorn and Boromir for Men.

The last two places are disputed. Merry and Pippin ask to go, and Elrond says they cannot imagine what lies ahead. Gandalf answers that neither can any of them, and argues for the hobbits: trust rather to their friendship than to great wisdom. Elrond remains in doubt and judges that Pippin should stay; Pippin says he will follow in any case, and Elrond yields. The Company of nine is complete, and none of its members has been drafted. Each has been called, and each has said yes.

⚡ FIRST READ
Elrond judges that Pippin should stay, then lets him go. What does this show about how the Company forms?
📚 PART 2 · NO OATH OR BOND

At the departure, Elrond gives his last word. Only one member carries a charge: On him alone is any charge laid. The others are free: The others go with him as free companions, to help him on his way. He adds a limit on what he asks of them: no oath or bond is laid on you to go further than you will. No one can know the strength of his heart before the road tests it.

Gimli answers that to say farewell as the road darkens is faithless: Faithless is he that says farewell when the road darkens. Elrond does not rebuke him. He says: let him not vow to walk in the dark, who has not seen the nightfall. Gimli replies that a sworn word may strengthen a trembling heart, and Elrond answers that it may also break it. The exchange puts two views of loyalty side by side. One trusts a vow. The other trusts a person to keep to a purpose he has understood.

The Company is therefore neither chosen like a club nor handed down like an inheritance. Each member is called, and each answers from a mix of three things: a personal purpose, loyalty to a person or a people, and a higher call to do what is right. The call is genuine, and it cannot be enforced.

📚 PART 3 · THE ROAD SOUTH AND THE PASS

The Company leaves at dusk in late December and keeps to night marches. Its hope is in secrecy, not war. Boromir blows his horn as they leave and Elrond tells him to wait until he is at the border of his land. Boromir answers: I will not go forth as a thief in the night. The scene shows the same man twice: proud, and open.

After a fortnight the Company reaches Hollin, a country of holly trees where Elves once lived. Gimli names the mountains that rise ahead and says the land of his fathers lies beneath them. Aragorn notices an unnatural silence, and then flocks of crows, the crebain, pass overhead as if spying. The Company moves on by night.

At the foot of Caradhras, Gandalf and Aragorn argue about the road. Aragorn says he thinks no good of the course, but there are no other passes until the Gap of Rohan, and he does not trust it since Saruman’s news. Aragorn begs Gandalf to say nothing of a dark and secret way. They choose the pass. Snow and a storm come on, and voices and stones seem aimed at them. Boromir wonders whether the Enemy governs the storm. Gimli recalls that Caradhras was called the Cruel, and later says the mountain has not forgiven them. Aragorn says he calls it the wind, but adds that many unfriendly things in the world have purposes of their own and are not in league with Sauron.

Gandalf lights a fire at the cost of revealing himself. Legolas runs over the snow, and Boromir and Aragorn force a path through the drift, carrying the hobbits. The Company escapes, but the chapter ends on a plain judgment: Caradhras had defeated them.

⚡ TRY IT
Choose the reading the chapter best supports.
1. Why does Elrond tell the Company that no oath or bond is laid on them?
2. Boromir hears fell voices in the storm, and Aragorn says he calls it the wind. What does Aragorn allow?
🔑 KEY TERMS
charge: a duty laid on someone; here, the task that falls to the Ring-bearer alone.
oath: a solemn promise that binds the one who swears it.
companion: a person who travels or works alongside another by his own will.
