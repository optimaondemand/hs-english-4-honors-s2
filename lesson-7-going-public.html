Finish
What finished means for your medium
●
Finished means every part is there, in role, addressed to your audience, and it looks and sounds like your world. Care and finish are what count here, not equipment. A careful phone recording can be finished. A polished piece with a missing part is not.

If you have not chosen a medium, go back to Lesson 2 and choose one before you continue.

Finished means: three scripted episodes, 6 to 10 minutes each, on camera, in role, with a page that lists your sources.
Finished means: two episodes of 15 to 20 minutes, or one of 30, in role, with show notes and a sources page.
Finished means: 2,500 to 3,500 words with a headline, an opening summary, pull quotes, images with permission, and your sources at the end, published as a real web page that Claude Code builds from your finished text. Claude does layout only. Every word is yours.

Post it to the class gallery
One post in The Public Square, addressed to your audience
●
The class gallery is a discussion in Canvas called The Public Square, where every honors student's finished work is posted for classmates to see. Your post carries the work or a link to it. The first three lines name your world, your role, and your audience. Then comes one line addressed to your audience, never to the class and never to your teacher.

Your accuracy note
What in your work is documented, inferred, invented, and answered
●
Your accuracy note is a short public note published with your work. It tells readers what in it is documented, what is inferred, what is invented, where the other side speaks, and what you had permission to use. Write it for your audience, not your teacher. It goes into your portfolio as "A note on accuracy." Write it here, then paste it into the template, ITW_AccuracyNote.docx.
