📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Three friends already know the secret, and their care does something care in this book does not always do: it leaves the decision with Frodo.
Chapter 5 reverses the long secrecy of Chapter 3. Merry, Pippin and Sam have watched Frodo for a year, and they arrive at Crickhollow with a plan. The scene can be set beside two other kinds of care in the same chapters, and beside the Samsa household.

📚 PART 1 · WHO KNEW WHAT

In Chapter 4 Frodo tells himself that taking friends on a road like this one is a different thing from taking them on a long walk. He decides that his inheritance is his alone, and that he should not even take Sam. Chapter 5 undoes the decision. After supper at Crickhollow Merry says he can tell why Frodo is miserable, since Frodo does not know how to say good-bye. Pippin lists the clues, from the muttering about whether he will see the valley again to the sale of Bag End. Merry reveals that he has known about the Ring for years, from the day, a year before the party, when he saw Bilbo vanish on the road to avoid the Sackville-Bagginses.

The conspiracy has parts. Merry has stores and five ponies ready in a stable across the fields. Sam has been the informer, and he has also been told by Gandalf that Frodo should take someone he can trust. Fatty Bolger is not going, and his job is to stay in the house at Crickhollow and keep up the pretence that Frodo still lives there. The plan arranges the Shire’s loyalty in the only way a small, settled people can: some go, some hold the fort.

🤝 THREE KINDS OF CARE IN CHAPTERS 3 TO 5
FARMER MAGGOT
Gives: supper, a ride in his waggon, a basket of mushrooms.
Advises: stay in Buckland and keep out of strange business.
Who decides: he assumes the safe course is back inside the Shire.
GILDOR
Gives: a night’s shelter, counsel, a blessing, the name Elf-friend.
Advises: go soon, and do not go alone.
Who decides: he says the choice to go or wait is Frodo’s.
THE FRIENDS
Give: ponies, stores, and themselves.
Advise: nothing; they announce that they are coming.
Who decides: Frodo finally yields, and the road remains his.

Frodo’s first answer is that he must go and that they should help him rather than hinder him. The friends do not argue that he is wrong. Merry states the limit of their loyalty: you cannot trust us to let you face trouble alone (Book I, Chapter 5). Pippin’s reasoning is just as compact, and part of it runs and therefore we must, too. (Book I, Chapter 5) Frodo laughs, calls them deceitful scoundrels, and gives in. His own words afterward are the best evidence of the kind of care it is: I cannot help feeling happy; happier than I have felt for a long time. (Book I, Chapter 5)

📚 PART 2 · CARE THAT FREES, AND ITS LIMITS

The care in these chapters has a shape. Maggot’s is generous and practical, and it points Frodo back toward safety inside the old limits. Gildor’s gives counsel and returns the decision to him. The friends’ does something different. They do not take the Ring, they do not choose the route, and they do not try to keep him. What they take away is his solitude, and with it the temptation to treat the burden as his alone. Their care widens his options, and he still decides.

The scene is not a perfect model of consent. Sam eavesdropped, Merry read a private book, and the friends spied for a year without telling Frodo. Frodo laughs it off, but the text does not pretend the methods were innocent. A fair statement is that the care frees and also presumes. It is generous in its aim and a little invasive in its means.

⚡ TRY IT · WHAT DOES THE EVIDENCE SHOW?
Choose the reading the text best supports.
1. Merry says Frodo can trust them to keep his secrets but not to let him face trouble alone. What does the distinction show?
2. Farmer Maggot gives them supper, a ride, and mushrooms, and tells Frodo to stay in Buckland. What kind of care is it?
3. In the Samsa household the care given to Gregor narrows his life. How does the hobbits’ care differ?
🔑 KEY TERMS
conspiracy: a secret plan made by a group; here a friendly one.
pretence: a false show kept up to hide the truth.
loyalty: faithfulness to a person or cause, especially when it costs something.
consent: agreement given freely by the person affected.
