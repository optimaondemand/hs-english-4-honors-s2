The Door Opens
A mind no one can hear, and a father who drives him back
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Gregor opens the door, and the family sees him.
Inside, Gregor is the same son and salesman who wanted to keep his job. Outside, the clerk, the mother, and the father see only a creature. The lesson reads the gap between what Gregor means and what is perceived, and compares his reception with that of the creature in Frankenstein.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can describe the gap between Gregor’s intentions and how others perceive him at the door.
2
I can explain how the clerk, the mother, and the father each respond.
3
I can compare Gregor’s reception with the creature’s in Frankenstein.
📋 READING
▶
Reread the end of Part I, from Gregor turning the key to the moment he is shut back in his room.
📚 PART 1 · A MIND NO ONE CAN HEAR

Gregor turns the key with his mouth, since he has no hands, and opens the door. Before the door opened, the clerk had already remarked that the voice was an animal’s. The sight of Gregor settles the matter at once: the clerk cries out, presses a hand to his mouth, and backs away step by step. The mother, her hair still loose from bed, sinks to the floor. The father clenches his fists and then weeps.

Leaning in the doorway, Gregor still means to calm the clerk, to protect his post, and to spare his parents. He speaks at length, reasoning and pleading, and the narration stays inside his mind, so the reader sees these intentions. The people in the room do not hear them. The clerk turns away as soon as Gregor begins and edges toward the hall. No one in the room asks what Gregor is trying to say.

📚 PART 2 · DRIVEN BACK

As the clerk edges out toward the stairs, Gregor lets go of the door and pushes after him, believing the clerk must be calmed to save his position. He drops onto his many legs, and his body at last obeys him. The mother backs into the breakfast table and upsets the coffee pot. Gregor snaps his jaws at the spilled coffee, and she screams and flees into the father’s arms. When Gregor makes a run for the clerk, the clerk leaps down the stairs and is gone. The father takes the clerk’s stick, left behind with his hat and coat, and a newspaper, and herds Gregor toward his room, stamping and hissing.

In the doorway Gregor is too wide to pass. His side is scraped and injured. The father gives a hard shove, and Gregor is sent bleeding into his room. The door is slammed shut. The father who wept a moment earlier acts, once the clerk has fled, as though only the creature remains to be dealt with.

📚 PART 3 · WHO MADE GREGOR WHAT HE IS

Frankenstein offers a comparison. Victor makes the creature, runs from it at first sight, and refuses to take responsibility for it. The creature, who has taught himself to speak well, approaches the De Lacey cottage. The blind father listens kindly, and when the sighted family returns, they drive the creature out. In both books recognition depends on sight more than on speech.

Gregor differs in one respect: no one made his body. The question turns to what else made him. The debt to the firm bound him to his work. His earnings supported the household, and his mother describes a son who thinks only of the business and never goes out. The body changed overnight. The conditions that formed him did not.

⚡ TRY IT · WHAT DOES THE EVIDENCE SHOW?
Choose the reading the text best supports.
1. What does keeping the narration inside Gregor’s mind do during the door scene?
2. The father first weeps, then drives Gregor back. What explains the change?
3. How does Gregor’s reception compare with the creature’s in Frankenstein?
🔑 KEY TERMS
recognition: seeing and accepting someone as who he is.
intention: what a person means to do, as distinct from how others read it.
