1
Who holds what you need
Different keepers hold different things. Ask the right one.
●
Archives keep records. Museums keep objects and their histories. Libraries keep collections, and librarians who answer questions. Scholars know one thing deeply. Organizations keep their own records. You do not write to "history." You ask one specific thing of one right person.

How you will reach them
An email address or a form URL.
2
Write the letter, one paragraph at a time
Five parts, then it becomes a real letter
●
A professional letter has a date, the recipient's name and title, a subject line that says what you want, and a greeting: "Dear Dr., Ms., or Mr. Lastname." Each paragraph does one job. It closes with your full name and your school email.
A MODEL REQUEST PARAGRAPH, GENEVA WORLD
I am writing to ask whether the council registers from the 1790s held in your collection mention public concern over the experiments on animal electricity performed in that decade.
If such a mention exists, I would be grateful for a scan of the relevant page, which I would cite fully and use only for a school research project.
Subject line
Paragraph 1: who you are
A senior at Optima Academy Online, working on an honors project in English IV about , in the role of , addressing .
Paragraph 2: the request
The one specific thing you named in the last tab, asked plainly.
Paragraph 3: why this recipient
Why them, not someone else, and what told you they hold this.
Paragraph 4: what you will do with it
Say this is for a school project, how you will credit them, and that the use is course-only.
Closing line and signature
Your full name, Optima Academy Online, your school email.

4
What your audience actually believed
Two primary sources, in their own words
●
A primary source is something made in your world by the people who lived there: a speech, a law, a letter, a record, a picture, a newspaper. You need two that show what your audience believed about your question, not what a later writer says they believed.

Your sources list, and your first scholarly article
One row per source, and one article from a scholar
●
Your sources list is a Word template called ITW_Sources.docx in Canvas: one row per source, what it is, where you found it, what it gives you. You keep adding to it all semester. JStor is the library database the school gives you a login for. It holds articles by scholars. You need three across the semester, and this is where you log the first.
