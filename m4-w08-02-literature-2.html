Week 8
Boromir
Loyalty to Gondor cut off from a higher call becomes grasping.
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
A real loyalty becomes a danger when nothing above it holds it in check.
Boromir’s love of Gondor is genuine from his first appearance. His conversation with Frodo on Amon Hen shows a good aim turning into an attempt to take what is not his. The lesson follows the steps of that turn.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can trace Boromir’s loyalty through Chapters 6, 8, 9, and 10.
2
I can explain how his argument on Amon Hen moves from counsel to force.
3
I can state why the text presents the attempt as self-destruction and not as strength.
📋 READING
▶
Read Book II, Chapter 10, The Breaking of the Fellowship, as far as Frodo’s flight up the hill from Boromir. Review Boromir’s speeches in Chapters 6, 8, and 9.
📚 PART 1 · A LOYAL MAN

Boromir’s first stated aim is the defence of his city. In Chapter 6 he hesitates at the border of Lothlórien and says he would take A plain road, though it led through a hedge of swords over strange paths. In Chapter 8 he says, my way home lies onward and not back, and later: I shall go to Minas Tirith, alone if need be, for it is my duty.

He also serves the Company. In Chapter 9 he shouts the warning at the rapids, rallies the paddlers, and with Aragorn carries the boats around the portage. When Frodo plainly follows Aragorn, he yields: It is not the way of the Men of Minas Tirith to desert their friends at need.

The same chapters show signs of strain. He tells no one what Galadriel offered him. At the pavilion his speech slips toward the Ring before he corrects it. In the boat he sits restless with his eyes on Frodo. The text recalls that he spoke at the Council in a similar vein and then accepted Elrond’s correction. The change on the hill comes out of habits already visible.

📚 PART 2 · THE ARGUMENT ON THE HILL

Frodo goes up Amon Hen to be alone for an hour. Boromir finds him there, and his face was smiling and kind. He says he was afraid for Frodo and asks to stay and talk. The conversation falls into steps, and each one is a little further than the last.

🔄 HOW A GOOD AIM TURNS
StepWhat Boromir says or doesWhat the step shows
ConcernHe says he feared for Frodo and wants to help with counselA real care, joined to a wish to steer the choice
ArgumentHe says the strength of Gondor has long protected the hobbits, and he asks for a sight of the Ring againA turn from Frodo’s need to Boromir’s interest
JustificationHe calls the Ring a gift to the foes of Mordor and calls it mad not to use itA wish for power, presented as common sense
FantasyHe pictures himself leading armies and becoming a wise and kindly kingThe Ring supplies the imagined good it will do
DemandHe asks Frodo to lend him the Ring, then says it should be hisPersuasion has become a claim of right
ForceHe tells Frodo I am too strong for you, halfling and leaps at himA loyal man now willing to use violence on a friend

The narrator marks the change in his face: His fair and pleasant face was hideously changed. Frodo puts on the Ring to escape, and Boromir searches wildly, curses him, and falls.

📚 PART 3 · THE AFTERMATH

After the fall Boromir lies still and then weeps. He asks what he has said and then What have I done?, calls Frodo back, and adds A madness took me, but it has passed. The grasping and the horror are both real, and the text keeps them side by side.

Frodo’s judgment on the hill is plain. He tells himself the evil of the Ring is already at work even in the Company and says that Boromir has fallen into evil. When Boromir returns to the others he gives a partial account: he says he found Frodo, urged him to come to Minas Tirith, grew angry, and saw him vanish. Aragorn asks whether that is all, and Boromir answers that he will say no more yet.

📚 PART 4 · SELF-DESTRUCTION

The text distinguishes the attempt on the Ring from the sacrifice the Company mourned in Lothlórien, Gandalf’s stand at the Bridge. A sacrifice gives up something for a task that is larger than the giver. Boromir reaches for a power that would remove the Ring from its task and would also end the one hope the Council had named. His aim was to save Gondor, and the means would have destroyed it.

His loyalty to his own people is a real virtue, and the Council had already taught that no one people can carry this burden. Loyalty to a people needs a higher call to answer to. Without it, the best motive becomes a reason for taking. The scene also adds to the lesson of the Mirror: the Ring finds the wish that is already there and gives it a plausible argument.

⚡ TRY IT · WHAT DOES THE EVIDENCE SHOW?
Choose the reading the text best supports.
1. Boromir tells Frodo he may say that Boromir was too strong and took the Ring by force. What does the offer show about his state of mind?
2. After Frodo vanishes, Boromir curses him, falls, then weeps and cries that a madness took him. What does the sequence show?
3. Which description best separates Boromir’s conduct on Amon Hen from Gandalf’s stand at the bridge?
🔑 KEY TERMS
grasping: reaching for power or possession beyond what is rightly one’s own.
self-destruction: ruin brought on a person or a cause by that person’s own act.
justification: a reason offered to make an act seem right.
