Week 8
The Breaking
Frodo goes on alone for everyone’s sake, and Sam will not let him.
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
The Company breaks, and the call it served does not.
Frodo chooses to go alone to spare the others the Ring. Sam refuses to be left. The chapter ends with two figures on the river and a Company scattered in the woods, which sets the self alone against the self with others.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can explain what Frodo sees on the Seat of Seeing and what he decides.
2
I can compare the proposals the Company makes about Frodo’s road.
3
I can explain how Sam’s choice differs from Frodo’s plan, and how both differ from Gregor’s isolation.
📋 READING
▶
Read the rest of Book II, Chapter 10, The Breaking of the Fellowship, in your copy, from Frodo on the summit of Amon Hen to the end of the chapter.
📚 PART 1 · THE HOUR ALONE

Aragorn tells the Company that the choice has come and that the Bearer must make it. He declines to advise Frodo. Frodo asks for an hour. He later tells Boromir that he knows what he should do: I know what I should do, but I am afraid of doing it.

After Boromir’s attempt Frodo reaches the Seat of Seeing and sees the whole of the war. He sees the lands in motion and Minas Tirith far away and beautiful, and his hope leaps. His eye is drawn east to Barad-dûr, and all hope leaves him. The Eye becomes aware of him and presses toward him. A second thought comes from another source and tells him to take off the Ring.

The text states what happens in him at that moment. He is free to choose, and he takes the Ring off. His decision follows at once: the evil of the Ring is at work in the Company, the Ring must leave them, and he will go alone. He lists his reasons. Some of the Company he cannot trust, and those he trusts are too dear to him, so he will not take them into danger. He also judges that Aragorn is needed in Minas Tirith. He puts on the Ring again and goes down the hill unseen.

⚡ FIRST READ
Which choice in the chapter looks hardest?
📚 PART 2 · THE COMPANY WHILE HE IS AWAY

While Frodo is gone the Company sits at the river and debates. Aragorn says that none of them can say which way he would choose in Frodo’s place. Legolas suggests calling Frodo back and voting, and Gimli agrees. Gimli adds a statement that echoes the Council: none of us is under any oath or command to seek Mount Doom. Even so, he cannot leave Frodo.

Aragorn proposes a smaller party of Sam, Gimli, and himself for the eastern road, with Boromir returning home. Merry and Pippin protest that they cannot let Frodo go to Mordor, and that they cannot leave him. Sam sees what the others miss: Frodo is not hesitating between roads. He is afraid, and if he finds the courage he will want to go alone.

Aragorn answers the proposals to stop him with a statement about limits: I do not think that it is our part to drive him one way or the other.

Boromir returns without Frodo and says little. Aragorn asks if that is all he has to say. The Company scatters in panic, and Aragorn sees what is happening: We shall all be scattered and lost.

📚 PART 3 · FRODO AND SAM

Sam reasons his way to the boats. He is sure that Boromir is not lying but has not told everything, that something scared Frodo into deciding, and that Frodo cannot cross a river or a waterfall without a boat. He runs to the shore, sees a boat sliding into the water, and leaps after it. He falls in and sinks, and Frodo pulls him out by the hair.

The exchange is short. Frodo says It would be the death of you to come with me, Sam and that he could not bear it. Sam answers that he is coming and that he will not be left behind, and adds that otherwise he will damage every boat first: I’ll knock holes in all the boats first. Frodo laughs. His plan is spoiled, and he is glad. He says It is plain that we were meant to go together.

Frodo gives way to Sam’s will, and the consent is glad rather than grudging. Sam has his gear ready already, because he expected them to leave that day. The two cross the river to the southern slopes of Amon Lhaw, hide the boat, and set out over the hills into the Land of Shadow. The Company is left searching the woods behind them.

📚 PART 4 · ALONE AND WITH OTHERS

Frodo’s solitude is chosen on behalf of others. He takes the Ring away from the Company so that it can do no more harm there. Aragorn had said it was not the Company’s part to drive him, and the chapter treats that restraint as part of the answer, since the Council had said the burden could not be laid on another. Sam’s loyalty is also free. No one orders him and no one binds him, and the text says plainly that Frodo is glad of it.

The comparison with Gregor Samsa is a contrast. Gregor’s isolation is imposed by others and deepens as the family’s care thins. Frodo goes alone by his own judgment, and Sam answers at once with company. In both stories a person is cut off from others. In one the others close the door, and in the other a friend refuses to stay behind.

⚡ TRY IT · WHAT DOES THE EVIDENCE SHOW?
Choose the reading the text best supports.
1. Frodo decides that the Ring must leave the Company before it does more harm. Which reading of his decision is best supported?
2. Aragorn says it is not the Company’s part to drive Frodo one way or the other. What does this show about the Company?
3. Frodo meant to go alone, yet he gives in to Sam. Which statement best explains why?
🔑 KEY TERMS
burden: a heavy duty carried on behalf of others.
coerce: to force someone by pressure or threat.
companion: a person who goes with another by choice.
