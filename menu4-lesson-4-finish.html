What a complete draft holds

ANALYTICAL OR SYNTHESIS ESSAY
A complete first draft has a thesis, every body part, a counterargument, and a conclusion. Weak sentences can stay. Missing parts cannot.
DEBATE
A complete first draft has both sides at full strength and a judgment. If the debate is recorded, the full recording exists, even if rough.
LETTERS OR DIARY
A complete first draft has every letter or diary page and the afterword. Weak lines can stay. Missing pieces cannot.
SHORT FILM OR SCREENPLAY
A complete first draft is the full screenplay, or a full rough cut or storyboard with the planned sound. Weak shots can stay. Missing scenes cannot.
PODCAST OR ORATION
A complete first draft is the full script, or a full rough recording. Weak phrasing can stay. A missing section cannot.

Check it against the standard
Five rows, 100 points
●
Run down the core rubric and the craft row for the chosen format. Name the weakest row, and lift it.
THE CORE ROWS
Interpretation 25. Use of the texts 25. Depth 20. Craft 20. Completion and submission 10.
ANALYTICAL OR SYNTHESIS ESSAY: THE CRAFT ROW
A thesis stated up front. Logical structure. Quotations woven in: frame, quote, explain. A real counterargument, answered. Clean academic prose. About 900 to 1,300 words.
DEBATE: THE CRAFT ROW
Both sides argued at full strength and fairly. Each side grounded in the texts. A reasoned judgment that earns its conclusion. Written, or recorded as a spoken piece.
LETTERS OR DIARY: THE CRAFT ROW
A consistent, convincing voice for each writer. Every letter or entry tied to a real moment in a text. A clear arc that reveals an interpretation, closed by a short analytical afterword of about 150 to 250 words.
SHORT FILM OR SCREENPLAY: THE CRAFT ROW
Deliberate choices (framing, pacing, dialogue, sound) that carry the interpretation. A coherent scene or short film, or a screenplay of four to six pages. Production quality that fits student work.
PODCAST OR ORATION: THE CRAFT ROW
A clear spoken argument. Strong delivery (pace, emphasis, clarity). Awareness of the audience. At least one quoted passage read aloud to real effect. About 4 to 7 minutes.
