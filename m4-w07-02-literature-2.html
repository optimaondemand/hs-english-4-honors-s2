The Mirror of Galadriel
Frodo offers the Ring and Galadriel refuses: the test and what it shows.
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Temptation tests what is already in a person.
The Mirror shows each viewer something different, and the offer of the Ring shows Galadriel something about herself. The scene suggests that the Ring works on desires that were present before it appeared.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can describe what Sam, Frodo, and Galadriel each meet in this scene.
2
I can explain why Galadriel counts her refusal as passing a test.
3
I can state how this scene bears on the question of whether temptation reveals or creates corruption.
📋 READING
▶
Read the rest of Book II, Chapter 7, The Mirror of Galadriel, in your copy, from the Company’s night in the pavilion to the end of the chapter.
📚 PART 1 · THE FIRST TEST

After the audience the Company sleeps in a pavilion near the fountain and speaks of what the Lady’s look did to each of them. Sam says that he felt stripped bare, as if she were asking what he would do if she offered him a quick flight home to a garden of his own. Merry felt almost the same. Each found that he had been offered a choice between a frightening road and something he wanted, and that he had only to leave the quest to take it. Gimli adds that the choice felt private.

Boromir calls it a test or a temptation and says that he refused to listen, but he does not tell what he thought she had offered. He also presses Frodo to say what he saw, and Frodo declines. Aragorn answers Boromir’s distrust directly:

There is in her and in this land no evil, unless a man bring it hither himself.

📚 PART 2 · WHAT THE MIRROR SHOWS

Galadriel leads Frodo and Sam to a basin of water in a hollow garden and says that the Mirror shows the past, the present, and what may still come about. She does not advise Frodo to look or to refrain: Seeing is both good and perilous.

Sam looks first. He sees Frodo asleep under a cliff, himself climbing a stair, and then the Shire in ruin: trees felled, a red-brick building put up where the mill stood, his father turned out of Bagshot Row with a barrow. He wants to run home. Galadriel reminds him that he did not wish to go home without his master, and that The Mirror is dangerous as a guide of deeds. Sam decides: No, I’ll go home by the long road with Mr. Frodo, or not at all.

Frodo looks and sees a white-robed figure on a long road, so like Gandalf that he doubts whether it is Gandalf or Saruman. He sees Bilbo restless in his room, then ships, a river, a white fortress, and a white tree on a banner. Then the Mirror goes dark and a single Eye grows until it fills the basin. The Ring grows heavy on its chain and he slips forward. Galadriel’s voice stops him at the water.

📚 PART 3 · THE OFFER

Galadriel tells Frodo that she perceives the Dark Lord’s mind as he gropes for hers, and she shows him the ring on her own hand, Nenya, one of the three Elven rings. She explains what the quest means for Lothlórien: if Frodo fails, her people are laid bare to the Enemy, and if he succeeds, their power fades and their land will be swept away by time. Her only wish is that what should be shall be.

Frodo then offers her the Ring: I will give you the One Ring, if you ask for it. He explains that it is too great a matter for him. Galadriel laughs, calls the offer a courteous revenge for her testing of his heart, and admits the truth: I do not deny that my heart has greatly desired to ask what you offer.

She shows what the Ring would make of her. She rises in light, terrible and worshipful, and describes herself as a queen set up in place of the Dark Lord: All shall love me and despair! Then the light fades, and she is again a slender woman in white. She says: I will diminish, and go into the West, and remain Galadriel.

Sam adds a last, plain remark. He wishes she would take the Ring and set things right in the Shire, and he names what she would put right. Her answer does not dispute the aim: That is how it would begin. But it would not stop with that, alas!

📚 PART 4 · REVEALED, NOT CREATED

Three people meet one object here, and each shows what was already in them. Galadriel’s desire is not planted by the Ring: she admits that she had long thought about what she would do if it came into her hands. Frodo’s offer comes from weariness and courtesy, and he shows no wish for power. Sam’s wish for the Ring rests on a wish for justice, which is the good end that makes the Ring most dangerous.

The scene therefore gives a position on corruption. The Ring finds an opening in a person and does not make one. Galadriel passes because she sees the desire, names it, and sets it down, and she does so with full knowledge and with no one compelling her. The test measures what she already is.

⚡ TRY IT · WHAT DOES THE EVIDENCE SHOW?
Choose the reading the text best supports.
1. Galadriel admits that her heart greatly desired to ask what Frodo offered. What does the admission add to the scene?
2. Sam says he wishes Galadriel would take the Ring and put things right in the Shire. She replies that this is how it would begin and that it would not stop there. What does her reply show?
🔑 KEY TERMS
temptation: the pull of something wanted that a person has reason to refuse.
test: a situation that shows what a person will do or choose.
diminish: to grow less in power or size.
