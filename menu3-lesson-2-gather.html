Sacrifice or Erasure?
Quick Write · Week 3 · 20 points
●
This is the whole Quick Write. Write it in the boxes at the end of this section. Your teacher scores it with the table below.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Argue one reading of Gregor’s death in Part III, then answer the strongest other reading. Write 300 to 400 words.

●
Focus question. Is Gregor’s end a gift of himself, a loss of himself, or both?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
State your reading in the first sentence: self-sacrifice, self-erasure, or both. Say what you mean by each word.
2
Use two moments from Part III, such as the violin and the lodgers, Grete’s demand that the family be rid of the creature, Gregor’s last thoughts of his family, or the family’s outing afterward. Say where each falls.
3
State the strongest other reading fairly, then answer it.
4
Name one irony in the closing scenes (verbal, situational, or dramatic) and explain what it shows.
5
Use at least two of this week’s Word Study words, used accurately.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
The first sentence states a reading and defines its terms.
☐
Two moments from Part III are described accurately and tied to the reading.
☐
The strongest other reading is stated fairly, then answered.
☐
One irony is named by type and explained.
☐
At least two Word Study words are used accurately, and the writing is 300 to 400 words.

Alone in a Crowd
Quick Write · Week 4 · 20 points
●
This is the whole Quick Write. Write it in the boxes at the end of this section. Your teacher scores it with the table below.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Write an original scene of 150 to 200 words about a person alone in a public place, written close to that person’s thoughts as Mansfield writes Miss Brill. Add a note of about 100 words on the move you borrowed.

●
Focus question. How can narration stay close to a lonely person’s thoughts and still let the reader see what the person cannot?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Choose a public place and one person who is alone in it, such as a bus stop, a cafeteria, or a ball game.
2
Write 150 to 200 words close to that person’s thoughts, so their hopes color the narration.
3
Show what the person imagines about the crowd, and include one detail the person does not notice or understand but the reader does.
4
Use at least two participial phrases and two sensory modifiers.
5
Write a note of about 100 words that names the move you borrowed from Mansfield and says where it works in “Miss Brill”.
6
Use at least two of this week’s Word Study words, used accurately.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
The scene places one person alone in a public place and stays close to their thoughts.
☐
The reader can see something the person does not.
☐
The note names a specific move from “Miss Brill” and says where it appears.
☐
Two participial phrases and two sensory modifiers are in the scene.
☐
At least two Word Study words are used accurately; the scene is 150 to 200 words and the note about 100.
HOW IT’S SCORED
CriteriaFull creditPartialPts
The scene
8 pts		One person alone in a public place, rendered close to their thoughts, with a gap between what they imagine and what the scene shows.The scene reports from outside the person, or the gap is missing.8

Craft targets
4 pts		Two participial phrases, two sensory modifiers, and two Word Study words used accurately; lengths on target.Fewer than two of these, or off the length targets.4

PLAN FOR ANALYTICAL OR SYNTHESIS ESSAY
Plan three body sections. Give each one claim and one text moment, and place the counterargument before the last section. Aim for about 900 to 1,300 words.
PLAN FOR DEBATE
Plan two sides. Give each side three moments from at least two texts, then draft the judgment that decides between them. Decide whether it is written or recorded.
PLAN FOR LETTERS OR DIARY
Choose the writers (for example Grete, the chief clerk, Miss Brill, the boy in “Araby”, Marcus, or Emerson). Match each letter or entry to one text moment, and plan the afterword that names the interpretation.
PLAN FOR SHORT FILM OR SCREENPLAY
List five to eight shots or scenes. Tie each to a text moment, and note what the framing, sound, or dialogue says about the claim. Decide between a filmed piece and a screenplay.
PLAN FOR PODCAST OR ORATION
Outline three or four segments. Choose one passage to read aloud, and plan the spoken line that makes the claim clear to a listener. Aim for about 4 to 7 minutes.
