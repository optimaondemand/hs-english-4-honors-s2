Your craft standard and the year plan
What excellent looks like in the format you picked
ANALYTICAL OR SYNTHESIS ESSAY
A thesis stated up front. Logical structure. Quotations woven in: frame, quote, explain. A real counterargument, answered. Clean academic prose. About 900 to 1,300 words.
DEBATE
Both sides argued at full strength and fairly. Each side grounded in the texts. A reasoned judgment that earns its conclusion. Written, or recorded as a spoken piece.
LETTERS OR DIARY
A consistent, convincing voice for each writer. Every letter or entry tied to a real moment in a text. A clear arc that reveals an interpretation, closed by a short analytical afterword of about 150 to 250 words.
SHORT FILM OR SCREENPLAY
Deliberate choices (framing, pacing, dialogue, sound) that carry the interpretation. A coherent scene or short film, or a screenplay of four to six pages. Production quality that fits student work.
PODCAST OR ORATION
A clear spoken argument. Strong delivery (pace, emphasis, clarity). Awareness of the audience. At least one quoted passage read aloud to real effect. About 4 to 7 minutes.

Before you write
Week 1 · Who Made Gregor?
●
Three forces act on Gregor in Part I. Sort the evidence into the three columns before choosing a position.
THE CHANGE
The story opens after it has happened, and it is never explained. It sets the situation. It does not decide how the household treats him.
THE FIRM
Gregor travels as a salesman to pay off his parents’ debt to his employer. When he misses a train, the chief clerk comes to the house the same morning.
THE FAMILY
The family has lived on his earnings. They answer the change with alarm, and the father drives him back into his room.
6
Who Made Gregor?
Quick Write · Week 1 · 20 points
●
This is the whole Quick Write. Write it in the boxes at the end of this section. Your teacher scores it with the table below.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Write one argument of 250 to 350 words about who or what is responsible for what Gregor becomes in Part I. Weigh three candidates: the change itself, the firm he works for, and his family. Name your position in the first sentence.

●
Focus question. Who made Gregor what he is: the change, the firm, or the family?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
State your position in the first sentence: the one you hold most responsible, or how you divide the blame among the three.
2
Use Part I. Describe two moments by what happens and where, such as his worry about the missed train, the chief clerk's visit, or the father driving him back into his room. Quote a few words from your copy only if they carry the point, and cite the part.
3
State the strongest case for a different answer fairly, then answer it.
4
Write one sentence that gives Gregor's thought in the narrator's voice without a tag such as “he thought” (free indirect discourse).
5
Use at least two of this week’s Word Study words, used accurately.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
The first sentence names who or what is most responsible, or how blame divides.
☐
Two moments from Part I are described accurately and tied to the claim.
☐
The strongest other answer is stated fairly, then answered.
☐
One sentence uses free indirect discourse.
☐
At least two Word Study words are used accurately, and the writing is 250 to 350 words.
HOW IT’S SCORED

The Room and the Self
Quick Write · Week 2 · 20 points
●
This is the whole Quick Write. Write it in the boxes at the end of this section. Your teacher scores it with the table below.
THE ASSIGNMENT

Write 250 to 350 words about what the emptied room says about identity in Part II. Then name one object you would keep in an emptied room and explain why it is you.

●
Focus question. What do a person’s belongings hold of who they are, and what is left when they are taken away?
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
In two or three sentences, say what Grete and the mother do to the room and why.
2
Explain what Gregor’s clinging to the framed picture shows about how he still sees himself.
3
Choose one object you would keep in an emptied room. Name it and say why it is you.
4
Trace one object or image (the furniture, the picture, the food, the door) through at least two moments in Part II and say what it comes to mean. This is a motif.
5
Use at least two of this week’s Word Study words, used accurately.
SUCCESS CRITERIA (YOUR CHECKLIST)
☐
The clearing of the room is described accurately, with the reason for it.
☐
The picture is tied to Gregor’s sense of himself.
☐
A specific object of your own is chosen, with a reason that makes it you.
☐
One motif is traced through at least two moments in Part II.
☐
At least two Word Study words are used accurately, and the writing is 250 to 350 words.
HOW IT’S SCORED
CriteriaFull creditPartialPts
