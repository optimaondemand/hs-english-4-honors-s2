What Makes a Hero?
Frodo, Sam, Aragorn, Gandalf, and Galadriel compared, with Gregor recalled.
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Heroism in this book is a duty answered, and strength is not the common factor.
Five figures from Chapters 6 and 7 are set side by side, and Gregor Samsa joins the comparison. The question is what each is asked and how each answers.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can compare the five figures by what each is asked and how each answers.
2
I can separate strength from answered duty as a mark of heroism.
3
I can connect Gregor Samsa’s end to this definition and state where the comparison stops.
📋 READING
▶
Review Book II, Chapters 6 and 7 in your copy: Aragorn’s leadership at the blindfold, Galadriel’s words to Frodo at the Mirror, and Sam’s answer to what he saw.
📚 PART 1 · FIVE FIGURES

Frodo. He carries a burden he did not seek, and he is tested by the one person who could take it. Galadriel tells him she thinks he has courage and wisdom enough for the venture. He offers the Ring to her as too great a matter for him, and when she refuses it he carries it on.

Sam. His loyalty is small in scale and exact. After a vision that offers him home, he settles the matter at once: I’ll go home by the long road with Mr. Frodo, or not at all.

Aragorn. He leads while grieving. At the blindfold he ends a dispute by sharing the cost, and at the dell he apologises for forgetting that Frodo was hurt and tends both wounded hobbits himself.

Gandalf. He is absent, and the Company judges him through what he left. Frodo’s poem remembers him at the bridge: He stood upon the bridge alone and Fire and Shadow both defied. Galadriel’s verdict is that none of his deeds were needless.

Galadriel. She is the most powerful figure in the chapter, and her act is a refusal. She is offered the most power within reach and gives it up.

⚡ FIRST READ
Which figure looks least like a conventional hero?
📚 PART 2 · WHAT THE COMPARISON SHOWS
⚖ WHAT IS ASKED, AND HOW IT IS ANSWERED
FigureWhat is askedHow it is answeredWhat strength is involved
FrodoTo carry the Ring to its destructionHe offers the Ring away, and when it is refused he carries it onEndurance and steadiness; no force of arms
SamTo stay with his master on a road he fearsHe chooses the long road after seeing the costLoyalty and common sense
AragornTo lead the Company after Gandalf’s fallHe shares costs and tends the woundedAuthority held with care
GandalfTo bring the Company through MoriaHe stands alone on the bridge and fallsGreat power, spent for others
GaladrielTo refuse the Ring when it is freely offeredShe names her desire and declinesSelf-command over great power
GregorTo sustain a household through work and silenceHe keeps caring, hides himself, and dies without complaintQuiet care; no one recognises it

The table does not support the idea that heroes are the strongest. Gandalf and Galadriel are strong and Sam is not, and the text does not rank them by strength. Galadriel names the unit that counts when she tells Frodo that for the fate of Lothlórien he is not answerable, but only for the doing of your own task. A hero in this book answers the duty that is his own and does not take on the whole outcome.

Three tests recur across the figures: the duty is real, it is taken up freely, and the cost is understood. Sam and Galadriel each pass through a moment where the cost is plain and the way out is open. Frodo meets the same moment, offers the burden away, and takes it up again when it is refused.

📚 PART 3 · GREGOR RECALLED

Gregor Samsa belongs in the comparison because he also keeps a duty with no one watching. Before the change he works long hours to pay off his father’s debt, and after it he hides from his sister and watches the household as it strains. Near the end of Part III he hears his sister say that the creature must go, returns to his room, and dies in the night, with his last thoughts turning to the family he is leaving.

The comparison shows where Gregor differs. His duty grew out of the household’s need, and in time the family took it for granted. The people around him did not recognise it as a gift. Lothlórien mourns Gandalf aloud and names his deeds, while the Samsa household meets Gregor’s end with relief and an outing. Neither text settles whether his end is a gift of self or an erasure of self, and the comparison makes the question sharper.

⚡ TRY IT · WHAT DOES THE EVIDENCE SHOW?
Choose the reading the text best supports.
1. Galadriel tells Frodo that for the fate of Lothlórien he is not answerable, only for the doing of his own task. What view of heroism does this support?
2. Why does Galadriel’s refusal of the Ring count as heroic although she uses no force?
3. How does Gandalf’s fall differ from Gregor’s end as an answer to duty?
🔑 KEY TERMS
answerable: held to account for something; responsible for it.
duty: a task that belongs to a person by role, promise, or call.
recognition: acknowledgement by others of what a person has given.
