💡 THE SETTING

Rivendell is Elrond’s house, known as the Last Homely House, and it lies east of the Sea. Bilbo had described it long before as a perfect house, whether you like food or sleep or story-telling or singing, or just sitting and thinking best.

📚 PART 1 · A HOUSE OF HEALING

Frodo wakes on the twenty-fourth of October with Gandalf at the window. The wound, Gandalf explains, was worked on for four nights and three days. Elrond removed a splinter of the blade that had been working inward, and Frodo had carried it for seventeen days. The Riders had meant to pierce his heart and make him a wraith under the Dark Lord’s command. Gandalf credits fortune and courage together, and notes that the heart was spared because Frodo resisted to the last.

Gandalf also tells the end of the Ford. Elrond commanded the flood, and Gandalf added the shapes of white horses and riders. The Riders were swept away, but Gandalf warns that Ringwraiths cannot be destroyed so easily. When Frodo says that Strider was only a Ranger to him, Gandalf rebukes the word only: the Rangers are what survives, in the North, of the great people of the West.

Sam has hardly left Frodo’s side. When Sam comes into the room, he takes Frodo’s left hand and says that it is warm.

⚡ FIRST READ
Which statement best fits Gandalf’s account of why Frodo survived?
📚 PART 2 · THE FEAST AND THE HALL OF FIRE

In the evening Frodo is a guest of honour at a feast. Sam had asked to wait on his master and is told that this time he is a guest as well. Strider is absent from the table. When Bilbo later asks where he was, Strider answers that he often has to put mirth aside, and that Elrond’s sons had returned with tidings he wished to hear. The dwarf Glóin, seated beside Frodo, speaks courteously and reserves his own errand for the Council.

In the Hall of Fire Frodo finds Bilbo, who has been sitting and thinking and making up a song. The song of Earendil that follows is Bilbo’s own, apart from a green stone that Aragorn insisted on. An Elf, Lindir, cannot say which parts Bilbo wrote and which Aragorn did: It is not easy for us to tell the difference between two mortals. The welcome is generous, and it is also made across a gap of kind.

📚 PART 3 · THE RING AT THE FIRE

Soon after Frodo finds him in the hall, before the song, Bilbo asks in a whisper to see the Ring. Frodo feels a strange reluctance and draws it out. A shadow seems to fall between them, and Frodo sees not Bilbo but a wrinkled creature with a hungry face and groping hands, and wants to strike it. The music falters. Bilbo, who sees Frodo’s face, understands at once and tells him to put the Ring away: sorry you have come in for this burden. He adds a remark about stories: Someone else always has to carry on the story.

The shadow passes, and Bilbo asks for news of the Shire. The moment shows that the Ring bends the sight of its bearer even in the safest house, and that the one person who has carried it before is the one who recognizes the effect. Rivendell heals Frodo’s body. It does not take the Ring away. Earlier, at Frodo’s bedside, Gandalf had noticed a faint transparency about him, especially in the left hand, a sign that the change is not finished.

⚖ CARE GIVEN AND WHAT REMAINS
What Rivendell givesWhat remains
The bodyA splinter removed, the arm recovering, clean clothes laid readyA faint transparency in the left hand, noted by Gandalf
The welcomeA feast with Frodo as guest of honour, rest, and musicStrider and Bilbo both absent from the feast, each for his own reasons
The RingA new chain, a safe valley, a friend who understandsThe pull of the Ring, which turns Bilbo into a grasping creature in Frodo’s sight for a moment
⚡ TRY IT
Choose the reading the chapter best supports.
1. When Bilbo asks to see the Ring, Frodo briefly sees him as a wrinkled creature with a hungry face. What does the moment show?
2. What does Lindir’s remark about two mortals suggest about Rivendell’s welcome?
🔑 KEY TERMS
wraith: a ghostly figure; here, a person stripped of will and bound to a dark master.
guest of honour: a guest given the chief place at a gathering.
