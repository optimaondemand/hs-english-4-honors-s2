📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Chapter 2 is one long conversation, and Gandalf’s history turns a gold ring into a question about what each holder did with it.
The chapter explains the Ring, but it also explains a creature. Gollum is the clearest case in the book of how a person comes to be called a monster, and Bilbo’s pity toward him is the first sign that the Ring’s story is not only a story of power.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can trace the Ring from its making to Frodo, naming how each holder came to it.
2
I can explain what turned Sméagol into Gollum, using evidence from Gandalf’s account.
3
I can explain what Gandalf means by calling the finding of the Ring intended, and what the word leaves unsaid.

Read Book I, Chapter 2, “The Shadow of the Past”, in your copy.
📚 PART 1 · THE RING’S HISTORY

Gandalf tells the story in the order Frodo needs it. The Ring was made by Sauron, who put much of his own power into it so that he could rule the lesser rings. It was taken from him when Isildur cut it from his hand at the end of a great war in which two kings died. Isildur then lost it. Ambushed by orcs near the Gladden Fields, he swam the river, the Ring slipped from his finger, and he was shot.

The Ring lay in the river until a fisherman named Déagol found it. His friend Sméagol was watching, killed him for it, and took it under the mountains, where it stayed for ages until Bilbo found it in the dark. Before telling this history, Gandalf heats the Ring in the fire. Frodo sees fiery letters appear but cannot read them, and Gandalf reads the inscription that identifies it as the One Ring.

🔗 WHO HELD THE RING, AND HOW IT CAME TO THEM
Sauron
Made it, and put much of his own power into it.
Isildur
Cut it from Sauron’s hand and kept it. It slipped away as he swam.
Déagol
Found it while fishing. Murdered by his friend for it.
Sméagol / Gollum
Took it by force, hid under the mountains, and was consumed by it.
Bilbo
Found it in the dark. Spared Gollum, kept the Ring secret, and gave it up with Gandalf’s help.
Frodo
Inherits it. Learns what it is.

📚 PART 2 · WHAT MADE A MONSTER

Gandalf’s account of Sméagol follows a sequence. He wants the Ring because it is beautiful and because it is his birthday. He kills for it. He uses its power for small meanness, spying and theft. His family shuns him and finally casts him out. He retreats to the dark, hating the sun and the Ring in turn, until the Ring has taken over his mind.

The cause Gandalf gives is a deed, a habit, and an object, and the account does not begin with a change of body. Sméagol’s body changes afterward. This is the reverse of the order in Kafka’s story, where Gregor’s body changes first and the family’s conduct decides whether he is treated as a person. Gollum raises the question of how much of a monster is made by what he does and how much by what is done around him.

Frodo’s first response is disgust, and he finds the kinship with hobbits an abominable idea. Gandalf does not soften the facts. He says only that the story is sad and that it might have happened to others, even to some hobbits that I have known (Book I, Chapter 2). He adds that Gollum was not wholly ruined, since a corner of his mind was still his own, and that a kind voice in the dark, Bilbo’s during the riddle-game, had let some old light in.

The Ring itself is part of the explanation. Gandalf insists that A Ring of Power looks after itself, Frodo. (Book I, Chapter 2) In his account the Ring was not left behind by Gollum. It left him, because its master was stirring and it could make no more use of a creature so small. This makes the Ring something like a creature of its maker, with an aim of its own, and every holder a part of that aim without knowing it.

📚 PART 3 · PITY, AND A FIRST HINT OF A CALL

The Prologue already reports that Bilbo, with his sword in hand and Gollum at a disadvantage, did not strike. Gandalf now names the reason and gives it weight:

Pity? It was Pity that stayed his hand. (Book I, Chapter 2)

Frodo hears this and objects. Gollum has done terrible things, and Frodo thinks he deserves death. Gandalf’s answer sets a limit on judgment: Many that live deserve death. And some that die deserve life. (Book I, Chapter 2) No one in the room can restore the dead, so no one should be quick to hand out death. Gandalf adds that Gollum is bound up in the fate of the Ring and may have a part to play.

The same conversation offers the first hint that Frodo is called to something. Gandalf says the finding was not only the Ring’s doing and not the doing of its maker either: Bilbo was meant to find the Ring, and not by its maker (Book I, Chapter 2). He draws the conclusion that Frodo was also meant to have it. Later, when Frodo asks why it came to him, Gandalf says it was not for power or wisdom or any merit that others lack. The text leaves the source of the “meant” unnamed.

Frodo does not welcome the thought; he answers that it is not encouraging. Earlier, on hearing that the Enemy has returned, he has already said I wish it need not have happened in my time (Book I, Chapter 2). Gandalf agrees that no one chooses the times they live in, and he says what remains is what to do with the time that is given us (Book I, Chapter 2). Near the end of the conversation Gandalf refuses the Ring when Frodo offers it, warning that its way to his own heart would be through pity, and he says the decision lies with Frodo. Frodo decides that, for now, the Ring stays in his care, and that he must leave the Shire.

⚡ TRY IT · WHAT DOES THE EVIDENCE SHOW?
Choose the reading the text best supports.
1. Gandalf says the Ring left Gollum. What does this claim add to the Ring’s history?
2. Frodo says Bilbo should have killed Gollum. How does Gandalf answer?
3. Gandalf says the finding of the Ring was intended, not an accident. What is the most careful reading?
🔑 KEY TERMS
Sauron: the Dark Lord, maker of the One Ring.
Isildur: the man who cut the Ring from Sauron’s hand and lost it in the River.
pity: as Gandalf uses it, the decision to spare an enemy when killing him is not necessary.
defence: an excuse a person repeats to himself until he almost believes it, as Gollum does about the birthday.
