Alone Together
Gregor, Miss Brill, and the boy: being seen and being recognized
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
Three characters are alone while surrounded: Gregor in his room, Miss Brill in a crowd, the boy in a hall of strangers.
The comparison separates being seen from being recognized, and asks what belonging requires and who can give it.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can compare how Gregor, Miss Brill, and the boy are seen by the people around them.
2
I can distinguish being seen from being recognized, using evidence from each text.
3
I can state what belonging requires in these texts and who is in a position to give it.
📋 READING
▶
Reread the last page of “Miss Brill” and of “Araby” on their lesson pages. Review the end of Part III of The Metamorphosis in your copy.
📚 PART 1 · SEEN AND RECOGNIZED

To be seen is to be noticed and sorted: as a customer, a nuisance, an odd old woman, an insect. To be recognized is to be met as a person whose inner life counts for the other. All three characters are seen. Almost no one recognizes them.

In each case the people nearest could have answered the person and did not. The sister’s care for Gregor was real in Part II and had narrowed by Part III. The old couple on the bench say nothing to Miss Brill. The uncle in “Araby” is kind in manner and forgets the one thing the boy asked.

📚 PART 2 · WHAT BELONGING REQUIRES

Recognition runs both ways, and the three characters fail at it as well as suffer from it. Miss Brill sorts the regulars on the benches as odd and silent, and does not see that she fits the description. The boy treats Mangan’s sister as a figure of light and hair, and learns nothing of her convent retreat beyond what she says in one sentence. Gregor’s family sees an insect and cannot see the son.

Each character also tries to supply the missing company alone. Miss Brill writes the play and casts the crowd in it. The boy builds a quest and a promise. In Part III Gregor comes out toward the music, and the household answers with alarm and anger. In the first two stories an outsider’s ordinary remark or manner breaks the supplied company: the couple’s talk, the stall-keeper’s tone. The supplied company does not hold, because it has no one inside it who can answer.

⚡ FIRST READ
Which statement best separates being seen from being recognized in these texts?
📚 PART 3 · COMPANY SUPPLIED ALONE

Taken together, the texts suggest three conditions for belonging. First, someone nearby has to answer the person as a person, not as a role. Second, the person has to be able to see the others in the same way. Third, the exchange has to be mutual, so that no one has to imagine the other half. In all three texts at least one condition is missing, and in each the missing half is supplied by imagination: the play, the quest, or the hope of the household that the problem would go away.

This does not settle who is at fault. The characters’ isolation has causes that sit partly in them and partly in the people and places around them. The texts leave that balance open, and the evidence on each side can be listed.

⚡ TRY IT · WHAT DOES THE EVIDENCE SHOW?
Choose the reading the texts best support.
1. The couple on the bench sees Miss Brill. What does that seeing lack?
2. Miss Brill and the boy in “Araby” each hold an imagined version of events. What breaks it in each story?
🔑 KEY TERMS
recognition: meeting a person as someone whose thoughts and feelings count.
belonging: a place among others in which one can be answered and can answer.
isolation: separation from others, by circumstance, by others’ conduct, by one’s own habits, or by all three.
