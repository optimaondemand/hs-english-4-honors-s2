Revise with one piece of feedback
A fresh reader, then your biggest fix
●
Revision starts from one reader’s response. Ask a classmate, a family member, or your Claude account to read the draft and name the weakest spot.
Working with Claude. Claude can read a draft and point out where a claim lacks evidence, where a paragraph wanders, or where a transition is missing. It cannot tell whether your reading of the texts is right, and it only knows the rubric if you paste it in. Check each suggestion against the text, and decide which to take. The interpretation, the choices, and the voice stay yours. Note where Claude helped.

5
Check it against the standard
Five rows, one last time
●
Run down the five rows of the rubric once, then the craft standard for your format. Lift one row before you submit.
YOUR CRAFT ROW: ANALYTICAL OR SYNTHESIS ESSAY
A thesis stated up front. Logical structure. Quotations woven in: frame, quote, explain. A real counterargument, answered. Clean academic prose. About 900 to 1,300 words.
YOUR CRAFT ROW: DEBATE
Both sides argued at full strength and fairly. Each side grounded in the texts. A reasoned judgment that earns its conclusion. Written, or recorded as a spoken piece.
YOUR CRAFT ROW: LETTERS OR DIARY
A consistent, convincing voice for each writer. Every letter or entry tied to a real moment in a text. A clear arc that reveals an interpretation, closed by a short analytical afterword of about 150 to 250 words.
YOUR CRAFT ROW: SHORT FILM OR SCREENPLAY
Deliberate choices (framing, pacing, dialogue, sound) that carry the interpretation. A coherent scene or short film, or a screenplay of four to six pages. Production quality that fits student work.
YOUR CRAFT ROW: PODCAST OR ORATION
A clear spoken argument. Strong delivery (pace, emphasis, clarity). Awareness of the audience. At least one quoted passage read aloud to real effect. About 4 to 7 minutes.
