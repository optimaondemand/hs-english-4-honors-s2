A father in uniform, and the household’s reversal
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
The father returns home and meets Gregor outside his room.
The scene ends with an apple lodged in Gregor’s back. Behind it stands a reversal: as Gregor weakens, the family’s strength returns. The lesson reads the scene and the shift beneath it.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can describe the father’s return and what the apples do.
2
I can explain the reversal between Gregor and his family.
3
I can discuss how the story links a person’s worth to usefulness.
📋 READING
▶
Reread the end of Part II, from the father’s arrival through the mother’s plea.
📚 PART 1 · THE FATHER'S RETURN

The father comes home and learns from Grete that the mother has fainted and that Gregor got out. He takes the worst view and blames the women. Gregor sees that he will be read as the aggressor, so he runs to his own door and presses against it to show that he will go in at once. The father does not wait to read such signs.

The man who enters is not the one Gregor remembers. Gregor had known him as a heavy man who received his son from bed or armchair, lifted his arms in greeting, and walked slowly on the family’s rare outings. Now he stands upright in a blue bank uniform, its buttons gold, the dress of the bank’s employees. His hair is combed flat and his eyes are alert. He tosses his cap onto the sofa and advances on Gregor with his hands in his pockets, lifting his feet high.

They circle the room slowly. When Gregor tires, the father fills his pockets from the fruit bowl on the sideboard and begins to throw apples. One glances off. Another lands squarely and lodges in Gregor’s back, pinning him in place. As his sight fails, he sees his mother run out of his room toward the father, her loosened skirts dropping to the floor. She throws her arms around the father and begs him to spare Gregor’s life.

⚡ FIRST READ
The father throws apples at Gregor. Which reading best fits the scene?
📚 PART 2 · PROVIDER TO BURDEN

At the start Gregor carried the household. His father had not worked for five years, since his business collapsed, and had grown heavy and slow. The mother suffers from asthma and spends many days struggling for breath by the window. Grete, seventeen, has lived a sheltered life of nice clothes, late sleep, and violin. Part II reports what follows: the family’s savings will last a year or two, and its members must earn. Gregor, listening at the door, grows hot with shame.

By the end of the part the father is in uniform, upright and severe, and he uses force on his son. As Gregor weakens, the others take on new strength and roles. The narration adds a hard detail: even before the change, the family had received Gregor’s money with gratitude but without much warmth in return.

The reversal puts the module’s question in an economic form: worth appears to follow usefulness. The family valued the provider, and when the provider is gone, the household reorganizes around its own survival.

🔑 KEY TERMS
reversal: a change in which two parties exchange positions.
provider: the member of a household whose earnings support the others.
