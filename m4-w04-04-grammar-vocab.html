If and Unless: Conditional Clauses
Grammar & Word Study · Week 4
📖 TODAY’S FOCUS
A conditional clause names a condition and the result that depends on it.
Reading these clauses correctly shows what a speaker promises, warns against, or only imagines. The sentences below come from the inn, the parlour and the road to the Ford.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can identify the conditional clause and the main clause in a sentence.
2
I can tell a likely condition from one that is contrary to fact.
3
I can write conditional sentences with the verbs in matching forms.
4
I can define and use this week’s Word Study words.
📚 PART 1 · CONDITIONS AND RESULTS

A conditional clause is an adverb clause that begins with a word such as if, unless or provided that. It cannot stand alone. It names a condition, and the main clause names the result. When the conditional clause comes first, a comma follows it. When it comes second, no comma is needed.

Condition key
if = on the condition that
unless = if not
likely condition: if + present verb, then will, may or can + base verb
contrary to fact (past): if + had + past participle, then would, could or might + have + past participle
contrary to fact (present): if + past form (were), then would + base verb
✎ ON THE BOARD · CONDITIONS AND RESULTS
If you go on to The Pony, you’ll find you’re not the only guests.
Likely condition. The clause If you go on to The Pony comes first and is followed by a comma. Main clause: you’ll find you’re not the only guests. The gatekeeper states a result the hobbits will meet.
If I had killed the real Strider, I could kill you.
Contrary to fact: Strider has killed no one. He states an impossible condition to show that an enemy would not stop to talk.
Unless Strider guides them, the hobbits will not reach Rivendell.
Unless means if not. Main clause: the hobbits will not reach Rivendell. Subject: hobbits. Verb: will reach, made negative by not.
Strider could have warned the hobbits before supper if the innkeeper had let him in.
The conditional clause comes second, so no comma. Contrary to fact: the innkeeper did not let him in, so the warning did not come.
If the hobbits trusted every stranger on the Road, the Ring would not travel far.
An imagined condition in the present. Trusted has a past form but refers to an unreal present; the result uses would.
⚡ TRY IT · CONDITIONS AND RESULTS
TRY IT: identify the clause, the kind of condition, and a word in context.
1. In “Unless Strider guides them, the hobbits will not reach Rivendell,” which statement is accurate?
2. In “If Frodo had stayed in the parlour, the Riders might have missed him,” which kind of condition is this?
3. Butterbur says his customers distrust anything “uncanny”. Which meaning of uncanny fits?
📚 PART 2 · WORD STUDY

These six words come from Book I, Chapters 9 to 12. Each entry gives the sense the chapters use, the root, and the chapter where the word appears.

uncanny (adjective) · strange in a way that unsettles; beyond the ordinary
Root: un- (not) + canny, a Scots word from can, to know; the sense “eerie” dates from the 1700s
Where it appears: Book I, Chapter 9
vagabond (noun) · a person who wanders without a settled home
Root: Latin vagabundus, from vagari, to wander
Where it appears: Book I, Chapter 10
inquisitive (adjective) · eager to learn things, often what is private
Root: Latin inquirere, to search into, from in- + quaerere, to seek
Where it appears: Book I, Chapter 9
perturbed (adjective) · disturbed in mind; troubled
Root: Latin perturbare, from per- (thoroughly) + turbare (to disturb)
Where it appears: Book I, Chapter 9
treacherous (adjective) · not to be trusted; dangerous in a hidden way
Root: Old French trichier, to cheat
Where it appears: Book I, Chapter 11
ominous (adjective) · suggesting that something bad will happen
Root: Latin omen, a sign of what is to come
Where it appears: Book I, Chapter 12
🌐 LOANWORD SPOTLIGHT · BORROWED FROM ITALIAN

Frodo travels under an assumed name. English borrowed a word from Italian for travel under a hidden identity.

incognito (Italian: “unknown”; from Latin incognitus)
At the inn, Frodo travels incognito and answers only to the name Underhill.
🔑 KEY TERMS
conditional clause: an adverb clause beginning with if, unless or a similar word that names a condition.
main clause: the clause that can stand alone and states the result.
contrary to fact: describing a condition that did not or does not hold.
