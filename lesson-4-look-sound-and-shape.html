Why your work cannot look like a template
The easy choice is rarely the true one
●
Most student media reach for a default: stock intro music, a template thumbnail, an old-timey font, a dark-fantasy palette. Your audience lives in a real place with real colors, clothes, letters, and sounds. Your work should look and sound like it came from there, not from a media library.

Choose your medium in Lesson 2 to see what these sources shape. Until then, gather them for the world itself.

The sound: three real sources
Music, ambient sound, speech
●
The same rule applies to sound. A tune from your world, a real ambient sound of the place, and how people actually addressed each other there all belong on your board, the same way your images do.

What this means for your medium. Your intro and your room tone should both come from these three sources, not from a stock library.
What this means for a feature article. A page has no soundtrack, so the sound goes into the words: the forms of address and the cadence your audience would recognize.
What this means for your medium. How you speak, where you pause, and the forms of address you use should come from these three sources.
